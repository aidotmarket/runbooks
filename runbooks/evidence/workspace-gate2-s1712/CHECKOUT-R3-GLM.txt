## Answers

### 1. Does the composite Order discovery reference plus the existing published Listing guard preserve both forged non-W optional isolation and registered-W drift retry/refusal?

**Yes, at design level.**

The proposed classifier uses only:

- fixed Order seller02/buyer01 pair;
- exact PaymentIntent;
- Listing seller ownership.

It removes all mutable Transaction filters from early classification. Therefore:

- the unchanged forged N case, whose listing remains seller01-owned, returns `False` before W schema or mount access and preserves HTTP 200;
- a matching seller02 listing charge reaches W readiness and provider-ID authority resolution before mutable product validation;
- missing readiness, runtime, mount, authority, or binding consistency raises `WorkspaceTestAuthorityUnavailable` into the durable failed/non-2xx path;
- exact absence with fully available compatible authority remains terminal suppression;
- an existing registration is validated and retains same-event convergence.

The composite reference then protects all early-discovery parent fields after registration:

- `orders.id`
- `seller_id`
- `buyer_id`
- `listing_id`
- `stripe_payment_intent_id`

`ON UPDATE RESTRICT` and `ON DELETE RESTRICT` prevent another value, NULL, or deletion after registration. The separate published-listing guard prevents `listings.seller_id` mutation while the W registration/publication exists, and FKs preserve the referenced row. Disabled or missing enforcement is catalog drift and must refuse readiness.

Mutable Transaction fields are intentionally not database-immutable. They move to post-authority validation and must produce durable 500/no-effect failure and same-ID recovery after restoration. This is the correct separation.

### 3. Does terminal closure correctly include NULL-linkage signed siblings, one-sided ambiguity, finite READ COMMITTED cutoff, and multiple valid replay events?

**Yes.**

The relevant-event union is conservative:

- linkage by registered order;
- linkage by registered PI;
- linkage by registered charge;
- OR signed payload relation through PI or object ID.

Thus:

- a signed failed sibling with NULL linkage remains visible;
- a one-sided or contradictory linkage is ambiguous and refuses closure;
- malformed matching signed evidence refuses closure;
- unsigned metadata is not used as proof;
- provider IO is not required for discovery;
- all retained Refund rows must have known resolved outcomes;
- exactly one normal full applied Refund must account for the registered amount;
- every visible relevant event must be resolved and consistently bound;
- multiple valid applied replay event IDs may point to the same sole Refund without creating another financial effect.

The deterministic representative is well defined:

- choose the smallest verified `stripe_event_id` under PostgreSQL `COLLATE "C"`;
- retain it unchanged on retries, even if a later replay sorts earlier;
- do not invent a Refund-to-event foreign relationship.

The READ COMMITTED cutoff is honest:

- observation is finite, sorted, bounded at 4096 plus sentinel;
- event rows are not locked after Order/money locks;
- visible committed admitted/failed/unresolved work prevents closure;
- uncommitted or later work is late delivery, not a timeless exhaustiveness claim;
- retained authority permits normal exact replay after closure;
- terminal receipt immutability prevents rewriting the selected representative.

This avoids reversed lock ordering and does not overclaim future event prevention.
