## 4. Answers to the six feared-failure questions

**Q1 — Can old/other-window/P/N/changed-purchase authority gain signed W admission, or a terminal unregistered refund be fabricated into convergence?**
No, by design. Admission requires a committed immutable `workspace_refund_test_authorities` row (PK `order_id`, UNIQUE `window_id/transaction_id/payment_id/payment_intent_id/charge_id`, immutable trigger rejecting every UPDATE/DELETE), bound to the exact `order→purchased_version→SellerListingPublication→DeliveryAuthority→seller-owned CloudConnection` chain with canonical hashes compared on every use. P/N live in the **separate** `s1681_refund_test_authorities` table with different CHECK constraints; the peer predicates `admits_s1681_negative_refund/failed_run_refund` (`webhooks.py:560-561`) are distinct and mutually exclusive with W — W cannot satisfy either, neither can satisfy W. An unregistered W charge is **terminally** suppressed (`e2e_scope_suppressed`), not admitted; R4 correctly *separates* `recovery_required_unregistered_refund` (terminal, no convergence path, escalate — "Do not retry the suppressed event to manufacture registration, bind retroactively, clear the incident or close the window") from `recovery_required_extra_order` (only the originally registered refund converges). A qualifying-but-unready W charge **raises** `WorkspaceTestAuthorityUnavailable`, which lands in the real critical path (`webhooks.py:548` try → `:868-870` rollback + HTTP 500), i.e. durable retryable failure, never false suppression. Row locks are existing-row `SELECT FOR UPDATE NOWAIT` in `order→transaction→order_money_states→Payment→Refund` order with the event mutex retained and no `lock_order_money_sync` creation. **No blocking finding** (see NIT-1 for disjunct-position ambiguity).

**Q2 — Can either cleanup service or a concurrent purchase/binder destroy history/cardinality? Is any reachable supported destructive path missed?**
No. Both `SurgicalResetService.reset` and `TeardownService.teardown` now refuse the four fixed UUIDs unconditionally via one pure helper, each placed before any destructive work: reset between apply-review (`:123`) and `_execute` (`:234`); teardown between `target_email` assignment (`:155`) and `validate_inventory` (`:156`) — verified exact. Both refuse for dry-run and apply, for direct service calls and routes (guard is in the service method), yielding zero destructive DELETEs / zero object-deleter calls (audit/nonce writes still permitted). Refusal is independent of any W table/mount/pointer/audit/window state, so protection holds before/during/after issuance and under missing/damaged lineage. **No third reachable supported account-destructive path is missed:** `app/e2e/` has exactly these two cleanup services and `e2e_reset.py` exposes exactly two destructive routes. R4 correctly caveats it does not claim protection against privileged arbitrary SQL, future unguarded routes, or authorized Docker-volume destruction, and mandates upgrade-time inventory of new cleanup entrypoints. Stage A/B concurrency uses bounded `orders SHARE NOWAIT` + row `FOR UPDATE NOWAIT` + `LOCAL lock_timeout=250ms/statement_timeout=2000ms/idle_in_transaction=2000ms` + 5s host deadline, rolling back with zero authority effects on `55P03/40P01/57014`/deadline. **No blocking finding.**

**Q3 — Does final TEST synthetic checkout work under the reviewed settings while both cleanup services stay protective?**
Yes. `checkout.py:192` gates synthetic purchase on `E2E_TEST_ROUTES_ENABLED`; flag=false → 403 `synthetic_purchase_requires_e2e_guard` (`:195`), flag=true → permits through normal financial processing. The reviewed Compose sets that flag true with **literal empty** allowlists; the empty allowlist is defense-in-depth (guard context refuses non-allowlisted targets), while the four-UUID service refusal is the **primary**, unconditional protection that holds even if a test deliberately populates the allowlists (the guard is a pure membership check, not allowlist-derived). `runtime_settings` (3 keys) lives in the mounted `runtime.json` receipt, separate from the 8-key `pins`, bound by persisted bytes/hash; the host checks effective `Config.Env` and the backend compares application settings to persisted `runtime_json`/hash. Route/checkout functionality does **not** depend on `E2E_ARMED_AT` (not referenced in checkout/e2e_reset/router). **NIT-2** only: the reviewed Compose should also set `E2E_ARMED_AT` to avoid the sysadmin `e2e_armed_window` fail-closed monitor alert. **No blocking finding.**

**Q4 — Can activation/recovery/malicious caller/artifact substitution duplicate resources, overwrite generation/Party, or leak a new Workspace secret?**
Not within the private cooperating-operator trust boundary (which R4 correctly declares as the boundary — a hash/UID/flock does not authenticate a malicious operator). Custody performs **no** DELETE/PATCH/upsert (create-once with exact GET reconciliation; drift/uncertainty → `recovery_required`/`deactivation_incomplete`, never overwrite); the golden digest for the synthetic 32×`A` secret (`22a48051…`) and exact markers/idempotency keys are fixed and independently mutated in tests. Party protection: the reassigning upsert must never run before the "no foreign Connect identity" proof, and projection + owned-identity creation are one transaction; no broad seed helper (`ensure_local_users/establish_parties_and_assertions`) is called. No new Workspace credential enters via Compose/argv/temp file; the child-boundary canary asserts the plaintext appears nowhere. Design-sound; enforcement is by the mandated subprocess-canary and PostgreSQL tests at build. **No blocking finding.**

**Q5 — Are schema/lifecycle/readiness/target files and all immutable columns concrete, with no invented model field or inserting money helper?**
Yes. Every registration-receipt binding maps to a real, correctly-typed column (verified above): `manifest_hash` on both `ListingVersion` and `AgentProposal` are distinct columns, so R4's new disambiguation test ("only the ListingVersion joined through `Order.purchased_version_id` supplies this binding") is meaningful; `review_hash` ≠ `request_hash` are distinct on both approval and publication, so "never substitute request_hash for review_hash" is enforceable; `immutable_scope_hash`, `approval_id`, `authority_id`, `listing_version_id`, `listing_id` all exist as unique FKs. Normal capture/refund/payout services remain the sole financial writers; W never manufactures capture. ## SIMPLER

None found beyond what R4 already adopts — and R4 is itself the simplification. The protection is now a single pure `is_protected_workspace_fixture(target_user_id)` membership check applied at two service entrypoints; R4 explicitly **removes** the R2 `pg_try_advisory_xact_lock(1712,17002)` and the strict-destructive fixed-party branch as unnecessary (the four-target path never reaches deletion), which is the correct minimization — confirmed reachable-path-safe because `reset()`/`teardown()` are the sole deletion entrypoints and the guard precedes `_execute`/`_execute_deletes`. The "no new tables" alternative for W admission was tried (`e138f16a`) and rejected on merits: unlike P/N, W has no amount-identical sibling to disambiguate, so persisted immutable authority is required; removing the tables would reintroduce the ambiguity the two-stage design exists to close. What I tried to remove — the second (window) table, the table-SHARE lock, the custody protocol — each is load-bearing for a distinct invariant (one-nonclosed-window, cardinality proof, no-DELETE retention). Advisory only.

## BETTER

One materially different approach: gate the synthetic W checkout behind a **new, narrower** flag that does *not* also arm the destructive `reset`/`teardown` routes, instead of reusing `E2E_TEST_ROUTES_ENABLED` (which imports both cleanup routes — `router.py:148-151`). This would shrink the armed destructive surface in the TEST environment. R4 explicitly considered and **declined** it ("A new narrower checkout flag is advisory and not selected; no unreviewed production flag change is implied"). The trade-off favors R4's choice: a new flag is a production-relevant config change carrying its own review/monitoring burden and divergence from the existing coupling, whereas the unconditional four-UUID guard already makes arming safe for the four fixtures — so the narrower flag reduces surface but does not reduce the residual risk the guard already eliminates. Failure modes: new flag → smaller blast radius but more config drift and a second arming signal to monitor; reused flag (chosen) → larger armed surface but a single, unconditionally-guarded invariant. Rollback is symmetric (config-only). Recorded as **advisory**, not a mandate — no scope violation or material risk supports elevating it.
