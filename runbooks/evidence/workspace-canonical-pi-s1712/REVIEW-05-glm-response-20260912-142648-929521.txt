## 1. Verdict

REQUEST_CHANGES

The review request itself was complete and the exact artifact identity reproduced. The candidate is a serious, mostly well-bounded design, and many hazardous alternatives were correctly rejected. It cannot be dispatched for implementation in its current form because the W refund authority is broader than the accepted exact-window authority, and the credential cleanup protocol still contains a non-atomic destructive name-DELETE race.

## 2. Findings

### 1. HIGH — The proposed W predicate admits any qualifying purchased Workspace order, not only the one order in the authorized owner window

**Evidence**

- `aidotmarket/runbooks@e138f16a6068ebc0214f7f11baa936f1dc4e689c:specs/SELLER-WORKSPACE-ACTIVATION-S1712-GATE2.md:55` defines the webhook predicate as a reusable purchase chain:
  `payment-intent/charge → order → purchased_version → SellerListingPublication → DeliveryAuthority → seller-owned CloudConnection`.
- The same candidate at `:57` says the host discovers exactly one new order against a complete snapshot, but immediately concedes:
  - “The signed webhook cannot use a host receipt as database authority.”
  - “This deliberately limits its exception by persisted purchase scope…”
  - Mars4152 “explicitly does not prove membership in a particular host window.”
- The candidate nevertheless excludes the missing persistence layer at `:55` (“no new financial or TEST-authority table”) and `:112` (“No…N-table extension is included. A reviewed choice to add W registration would require an explicit concrete extension before dispatch.”)
- The accepted product dependency is stricter: `aidotmarket/runbooks@26588d3571efc3a19d1a9b591fb51991690e3f62:specs/BQ-REFUND-SAFE-PRODUCTION-S1714-GATE2.md:151` requires that N and W “use separate exact persisted authorities, never actor-only permission.” Its test contract at `:165` explicitly requires altered bindings and cross-authority denial.
- Accepted Gate1 also requires the exact new Workspace order at `aidotmarket/runbooks@1ff494acc79245e8481ece2e985eda902fbf5207:specs/SELLER-WORKSPACE-ACTIVATION-S1712-GATE1.md:53`, and exact W cardinality at `:108`.
- Actual `aidotmarket/ai-market-backend@c3a62af8ccf60929a8e7cec3d62c9d30e0adefd2` confirms that the proposed chain has no owner-window or one-order discriminator:
  - `app/models/order.py:126-142,164` binds an order to buyer, seller, listing, payment intent, and purchased version, but has no owner-window reference.
  - `app/models/seller_listing_publication.py:12-22` makes publication/listing/version/authority bindings unique per publication, not per purchase window.
  - `app/models/seller_workspace.py:673-708` makes `DeliveryAuthority` unique by `(listing_version_id, version)` and carries no order/window.
  - `app/models/seller_workspace.py:140-146` binds the connection to a seller, not to one window or order.
  - `app/models/finance.py:287-295` makes Payment PI/charge bindings unique, which identifies one order but does not select the authorized window.

**Observed versus expected**

- Observed: the webhook predicate would accept any signed, full, captured TEST refund whose order has the fixed seller02/buyer01 actors and the same immutable Workspace publication/authority/connection chain. The host snapshot is evidence outside the database and is explicitly unavailable to the webhook.
- Expected: only the exact persisted order/transaction/PI/charge belonging to the explicitly opened W owner window may bypass synthetic suppression. Other W purchases, prior-window refunds, and later replayed W purchases must be refused by database authority.

**Reproduction — static evidence only**

Construct two persisted Workspace orders with:

- the same fixed buyer01 and seller02;
- the same listing, purchased version, publication, delivery authority, and CloudConnection;
- distinct order IDs, transactions, PaymentIntents, charges, and captures.

Both satisfy the proposed chain. The proposed predicate has no field by which it can reject the order outside the claimed window. The checkout’s current refusal of a second *active* purchase by the same buyer does not repair this: an out-of-window prior purchase, a later purchase after refund/release, or another compatible Workspace purchase is still within the predicate’s actual scope.

**Impact**

This violates the accepted “separate exact persisted authorities” contract. It can turn a legitimate signed TEST refund into an unauthorized out-of-window admission, and potentially another W order if duplicate or later compatible purchases exist. The normal processor will then faithfully apply the refund to the wrong-but-valid order. This is not an implementation detail; the candidate itself identifies it as a Gate2 decision and forbids MP from improvising the missing schema.

**Smallest required outcome**

Revise the specification and frozen scope to add one narrowly reviewed W authority registration before any implementation. At minimum it must specify:

- a table separate from `s1681_refund_test_authorities`, for example `workspace_refund_test_authorities`;
- immutable bindings for:
  - `operation_id`;
  - `owner_window_id`;
  - `order_id` as primary key;
  - unique `transaction_id`, `payment_id`, `payment_intent_id`, and `charge_id`;
  - fixed `buyer_id`, `seller_id`;
  - `listing_id`, `purchased_version_id`, `publication_id`, `delivery_authority_id`, and `connection_id`;
  - capture amount and lowercase currency;
  - backend source SHA, exact local image identity, environment SHA, accepted money-spec SHA, Workspace-spec SHA, and versions.env SHA;
  - canonical receipt digest and protocol version;
- constraints enforcing role W, one registered W order per owner window, and immutable binding columns after insert;
- terminal/refund state tracked without changing the authority binding;
- the exact migration and source files, including the backend migration/model, receiver registration mode, environment host command/action, webhook predicate, and tests;
- the lifecycle:
  1. activation under the existing owner lock;
  2. complete pre-purchase snapshot;
  3. W purchase;
  4. a reviewed `bind`/`register-purchase` mutation under the same lock where the backend receiver independently recomputes the complete order delta and chain, then commits the authority row;
  5. refund admission only when the signed event matches that immutable database row and all accepted pins;
  6. deactivation only after that exact authority is refunded/terminal and cannot settle.

The host receipt may remain diagnosis evidence, but must not be the webhook’s authority.

**Verification criterion**

A revised design must name the schema, migration, files, and lifecycle before dispatch. Implementation tests must prove, through the signed endpoint:

- the registered W order is admitted and processed exactly once;
- a second qualifying W order, prior-window order, later order, wrong PI/charge, altered stored binding, or conflicting registration is denied;
- P, N, unrelated synthetic actors, agent/device transactions, partial/zero/wrong amounts, live mode, and incompatible readiness are denied;
- revoking access or the CloudConnection does not prevent the authorized refund;
- replay converges to one set of financial effects;
- no active credential or download access is needed for admission.

### 2. HIGH — Infisical cleanup cannot establish exclusive custody; read-then-DELETE can delete another generation or party after the final read

**Evidence**

- `aidotmarket/runbooks@e138f16a6068ebc0214f7f11baa936f1dc4e689c:specs/SELLER-WORKSPACE-ACTIVATION-S1712-GATE2.md:75` correctly states that Infisical DELETE by secret name has no expected-version or UUID precondition and that read-then-delete is not compare-and-delete.
- The same section nevertheless proposes “exclusive ownership… for the entire operation” under the local fixture lock, immediate re-read, marker/version/digest checks, and DELETE by the fixed name.
- Cleanup makes that DELETE part of successful deactivation at `:105-106`: delete the exact item, then record `inactive` only after all cleanup evidence passes.
- The actual shared lock is process-local: `aidotmarket/money-path-test-environment@606267af80470b1d61c07d91cda30a0dc5200224:bin/check-settlement:308-343` validates token/ancestor/flock and local checkpoint state. It cannot serialize an independent writer using the Infisical HTTP API.
- The candidate itself says at `:75` to preserve the secret if exclusive custody cannot be established, but it provides no enforceable way to prove that no independent Infisical-capable writer exists.

**Observed versus expected**

- Observed: the design still has a destructive sequence:
  1. GET fixed name and verify UUID/version/digest/marker;
  2. independent writer changes or recreates the same name;
  3. DELETE by name is issued.
- Expected: no cleanup path may delete a value it did not inspect, and successful terminal state must not depend on an unverifiable global absence of external writers.

**Reproduction — static evidence only**

Interleave an external `POST`/`PATCH`/DELETE+create on the same fixed secret name after the design’s final authenticated GET and before its name-based DELETE. UUID/version/digest/marker checks were all valid at GET time, but the name-based DELETE can remove the newly written item. The local fixture lock provides no barrier to that writer.

**Impact**

This can destroy another generation or party’s credential while marking cleanup successful. That is exactly the cross-generation custody failure the review was asked to assess. Better create/re-read reconciliation and preservation-on-drift reduce earlier risks, but they do not close the interval between final read and DELETE.

**Smallest required outcome**

Under the documented API, remove destructive DELETE from the required success path. Choose one of:

1. **Safer accepted alternative:** retain the fixed seller02 TEST TOTP item after database restoration, report `inactive_with_retained_custody` or `deactivation_incomplete`, and permit a later activation only when the exact owned UUID/version/digest/marker still matches. This removes the non-atomic DELETE interval entirely.
2. **Only if actually supported and proven:** use a provider-enforced atomic ID/version precondition or a dedicated single-writer ACL. The specification must name the concrete installed API capability and permission evidence; reviewers must not assume a fictional CAS endpoint or broaden secret access.

If option 1 is selected, the candidate’s “prove fixed name absent, generate a new value every operation, then delete after cleanup” lifecycle must be revised accordingly.

**Verification criterion**

Implementation tests must inject an external write after the final pre-delete GET and prove:

- no DELETE is issued under the current API;
- the existing item is preserved;
- cleanup is explicitly incomplete rather than `inactive`;
- drift prevents subsequent activation;
- no plaintext appears in logs, audit, arguments, exceptions, or output.

If a real atomic precondition is later used, mismatched ID/version/digest/marker and deletion-response uncertainty must each cause preservation, not deletion.

### 3. MEDIUM — The runtime pin schema does not distinguish the locally built backend Docker Image ID from a registry OCI index digest

**Evidence**

- `aidotmarket/runbooks@e138f16a6068ebc0214f7f11baa936f1dc4e689c:specs/SELLER-WORKSPACE-ACTIVATION-S1712-GATE2.md:37` says the cross-repository manifest names a “built image digest and source label.”
- The receipt/pins use one field, `backend_image_digest`, at `:61` and `:65`, and only say the receiver compares it with the actual container image/source.
- The accepted environment explicitly treats these identities differently:
  - `aidotmarket/money-path-test-environment@606267af80470b1d61c07d91cda30a0dc5200224:README.md:39-46` distinguishes the AIM Data registry OCI index digest from locally built backend/frontend digests.
  - `bin/up:158-180` records the backend’s local `docker image inspect {{.Id}}` value in `.runtime/image-digests.json`, separate from the AIM Data OCI index.
- The accepted checker later compares the running container’s local `Image` value at `bin/check-settlement:3889-3896`.

**Observed versus expected**

- Observed: the Workspace receiver schema calls the discriminator only `backend_image_digest`; it does not define the extraction source or whether this is the local Docker Image ID, a registry manifest digest, or a multi-platform OCI index digest.
- Expected: the money-path activation gate must name and compare the exact identity kind, with independent validation of source SHA and local image ID.

**Reproduction — static evidence only**

Two implementations can both pass prose-level “actual image digest” language while comparing different values: one uses `docker inspect --format '{{.Image}}' <container>`, another uses a published registry index digest or image-label manifest. A mismatched locally built binary could then pass the wrong comparison, or the correct runtime could be rejected.

**Impact**

This weakens the pre-mutation runtime/source pin gate. Because W admission can affect financial state, digest-kind confusion is material even though Docker operations were not performed in this review.

**Smallest required outcome**

Revise the pins and receipt schema to use explicit fields, for example:

- `backend_source_sha40`;
- `backend_local_image_id` = the exact local Docker `sha256:…` ID from the inspected running container or locally built image;
- if applicable, a separate `backend_registry_manifest_digest` or `backend_oci_index_digest`;
- explicit “not applicable” handling when no backend OCI index is published.

Name the exact read-only extraction commands and forbid substitution of AIM Data’s OCI index for the backend’s local image ID.

**Verification criterion**

Implementation tests must independently mutate source SHA, local image ID, source label, versions.env, and any registry/OCI identity. Every mismatch must refuse before audit, credential, provider, or database writes.

## 4. Answers to the six feared-failure questions

### Q1 — Can the W exception admit another order, P/N, arbitrary synthetic actor, or out-of-window refund?

**Yes for another qualifying W order and out-of-window refunds; no for P/N or arbitrary actors as currently designed.**

The fixed seller02/buyer01 binding and immutable Workspace publication/connection chain exclude:

- Mars N/failed-run authorities;
- seller01 P/N purchases;
- arbitrary synthetic buyers/sellers;
- agent/device-backed transactions;
- fake lookalike chains;
- wrong PI/charge joins;
- missing capture;
- wrong amount/currency/live mode when the accepted conjuncts are implemented.

But the purchase chain is reusable across W orders and contains no owner-window discriminator. The signed webhook cannot use the host’s one-new-order snapshot, so the predicate’s actual database scope is broader than its operational intent. That is Finding 1 and is blocking.

The design is correct that refunds must remain possible after credential, access, or connection revocation: admission should validate immutable purchased rights and financial capture, not active download eligibility. A revised persisted W authority must preserve that property.

### Q2 — Can activation or cleanup overwrite/delete another generation or party?

**Create/projection protections are mostly sound; destructive cleanup is not.**

Verified strengths include:

- actual User Stripe readiness projections are strings, not booleans (`User:84-87`);
- existing TOTP encryption is AESGCM v1 and remains coupled to `TOTPService` (`TOTPService:238-264,317-336`);
- the existing Connect upsert really can reassign a `stripe_connect` identity to a new party (`stripe_connect_identity.py:49-145`), so the candidate’s pre-proof prohibition and exact owned-row transaction are necessary;
- seller02 baseline, exact restoration fields, unique auth_user binding, complete marker pagination, no broad seed helper, idempotency reconciliation, and no Party/auth-user deletion are all specified;
- the actual four-fixture seed contract preserves buyer02 and refuses gained seller02 state.

The unresolved failure is Infisical name-DELETE. Local lock ownership cannot exclude an independent API writer, and no atomic UUID/version precondition exists. As written, cleanup can inspect one item and delete a later item under the same name. That is Finding 2 and is blocking.

### Q3 — Can a wrapper bypass the settlement lock/guard, mutate before refusal, leak a secret, or silently repair audit state?

**No bypass is visible if implemented exactly; one identity ambiguity remains.**

The existing environment mechanics support the proposed ordering:

- `check-settlement:308-343` validates inherited token, ancestry, held lock, and guard state;
- `check-settlement:346-372` launches only an exact environment-bin path and propagates child exit status;
- `workspace-fixture` is not in the guarded-name set, so requiring the child to invoke `--guard seed` is necessary;
- actual `bin/seed` and `bin/reset` acquire the wrapper lock before local guard and later writes;
- `compose.yaml:90-96` mounts `./seed` read-only at `/s1656`;
- private stdin, no TTY, no `-e`, bounded duplicate-key-aware JSON, no generic pass-through, read-only status, atomic audit, and secret-canary subprocess testing are all specified.

No implementation exists yet, so these remain design obligations. The remaining blocking ambiguity is the undifferentiated backend image digest/local Image ID/OCI-index identity in Finding 3.

### Q4 — Can crash, retry, or deactivation lose a paid role or duplicate resources?

**The general crash architecture is sound, but the two authority/custody gaps prevent full certification.**

The design correctly requires:

- durable preparation before provider creation;
- complete provider marker enumeration;
- no fresh account after uncertain or expired idempotency;
- exact database restoration before credential cleanup;
- one transaction for projection/owned identity creation;
- inspect-after-commit recovery instead of creating another secret/account;
- no broad identity helper or Party/auth-user deletion;
- retention of provider accounts with history;
- refusal of destructive cleanup when payout/refund/financial aggregate is uncertain;
- explicit terminal W financial disposition.

Accepted backend behavior also supports durable refund admission before provider reads and atomic effects before HTTP 2xx (`refund_processing_service.py:324-371`).

However:

- without exact W registration, crash/retry cannot prove which W paid role is authoritative;
- without a safe custody decision, deactivation can destroy another generation;
- successful `inactive` currently depends on a cleanup step that cannot be made safe under the documented API.

Those are blocked by Findings 1 and 2.

### Q5 — Does the frozen scope implement all accepted Gate1 without overwriting F1 or normal money behavior?

**Not as written. The mount, fixture cardinality, transport, account configuration, and protected-state obligations are otherwise well aligned.**

Verified compatibility includes:

- actual read-only receiver path `/s1656/seed.py`;

The missing load-bearing target is the exact persisted W authority extension. It must be added explicitly with migration, model/registration surface, host action, webhook predicate, and tests. This is not a refactor suggestion; it is required by the accepted product Gate2 contract.

## SIMPLER

**Concrete simplest correction preserving every requirement:** make two reductions:

1. Replace the broad purchase-scope W exception with one narrow immutable W authority row and one exact `bind-purchase` receiver transaction. This removes the host receipt’s pretended authority role and removes the entire class of “another qualifying W order/out-of-window refund” admissions.
2. Stop deleting the fixed Infisical TOTP name. Retain it under explicit owned custody and mark deactivation `inactive_with_retained_custody` or `deactivation_incomplete`. This removes the entire final GET→external-write→name-DELETE race and the uncertain-delete/retry cleanup branch.

Quantitatively, the custody alternative removes every per-operation DELETE and the need to prove a negative about all external writers. After initial creation, repeat activation can require one exact GET instead of create/re-read/delete/reconcile. It adds one reviewed authority table/action and a non-clean terminal state, which is less risk than an unsafe destructive operation.

This is a mandate here because both reductions are supported by concrete scope/risk evidence, not merely preference.

## BETTER

**Materially better approach:** make the W owner window itself a backend-owned transactional state machine:

- activation records the immutable window and custody/provider discriminators;
- `bind-purchase` recomputes the complete order delta inside the backend transaction and commits the exact W authority;
- webhook admission joins only that authority and all accepted source/image/spec pins;
- the fixed TEST custody item is immutable and retained rather than deleted;
- deactivation requires the authority’s terminal refund state and records retained custody explicitly.

**Comparison**

- Failure modes:
  - current design: valid but out-of-window W refund admitted; external credential writer deleted;
  - better approach: database constraints reject unregistered/conflicting W orders; no destructive name-DELETE exists.
- Cost:
  - one migration and narrow model/registration action;
  - revised deactivation state;
  - focused PostgreSQL, subprocess-canary, webhook, crash, pagination, and pin-mismatch tests.
- Rollback:
  - disable the W admission predicate;
  - preserve existing paid rights and immutable authority rows;
  - quarantine/retain the custody item and audit rather than deleting or reassigning it.

This remains a narrow Workspace state machine, not the generic hook framework or broad secret/provider access the candidate correctly avoids.
