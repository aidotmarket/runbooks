## Answers to the six required questions

### 1. Can an old/other-window W order, P/N, or altered purchase gain signed refund admission?

**No, not through the proposed W authority, assuming the stated tests are implemented.**

The two-stage design closes the predecessor purchase-scope defect:

- Stage A persists a complete fixed-pair order-ID baseline under a short `orders SHARE NOWAIT` transaction.
- Stage B recomputes the complete current fixed-pair set and requires:
  - all baseline IDs still present;
  - the entire delta to contain exactly one order;
  - that order to be paid;
  - unique canonical order/transaction/capture/provider bindings;
  - exact actors;
  - positive USD full capture;
  - no pending payout, dispute, ambiguity, or pre-existing unregistered refund.
- The registration row is immutable and uniquely binds window, order, transaction, Payment, PaymentIntent, charge, listing/version/publication/approval/delivery authority/connection, actors, amount, protocol, pins, and canonical receipt.
- The webhook predicate locates authority by provider IDs and independently compares all scalar fields and hashes.
- P/N uses different actors and persisted authorities; it cannot satisfy W.
- A closed or recovery window can converge only the originally registered event/order and cannot bind a second order.
- The operator must receive the committed registration receipt before requesting a refund.
- A pre-registration signed refund is explicitly treated as a terminal suppression incident, not retroactively registered.
- The existing `lock_order_money_sync` insertion hazard is acknowledged and replaced by existing-row locks.
- Real signature, scope, dedup, and immutable event binding precede the W disjunct.

This is a design-level conclusion. The required real ASGI and PostgreSQL tests remain unexecuted.

### 2. Can public purchase, duplicate binder, or reset destroy protected history or defeat exact cardinality?

**The specific SurgicalReset and order-cardinality design is sound, but the overall answer is not yet fully safe because of Finding 1.**

Verified within the requested SurgicalReset path:

- The four packaged identities exactly match the proposed constants.
- Existing source permits a concrete guard after authentication/review and before execution.
- The refusal is independent of schema, mount, pointer, audit, custody, lineage, and window state.
- Dry-run and apply can both refuse before any destructive SQL or object-deleter call.
- The obsolete advisory/strict-destructive branch is correctly removed: a database advisory lock could not serialize host owner-file publication, and the four-target path no longer needs to reach deletion.
- Unrelated allowlisted targets remain outside the protected set.
- The separately guarded environment `bin/reset` lifecycle is distinct from the backend endpoint.

For cardinality:

- PostgreSQL `SHARE` conflicts with the `ROW EXCLUSIVE` lock acquired by order INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE.
- `NOWAIT` provides immediate refusal when a writer is already active.
- After SHARE is acquired, a later writer cannot insert or change order cardinality before commit.
- The design correctly acknowledges that preexisting `ROW SHARE`/`SELECT FOR UPDATE` locks are compatible and therefore uses later bounded NOWAIT row locks.
- Duplicate binders are serialized by the window row and uniqueness constraints.
- No order UPDATE or money-state creation is allowed during binding.
- Timeouts, deadlock, statement failure, and uncertain commit are reconciled through rollback and immutable identity.
- An extra order cannot gain authority and triggers the named preservation incident rather than deletion, synthetic refund, false closure, or timestamp/cardinality manipulation.

However, enabling the required synthetic checkout imports the separate destructive TeardownService not covered by the R3 guard. That is Finding 1 and blocks implementation until corrected or explicitly proven unreachable under the final runtime configuration.

## SIMPLER

**No simpler overall design preserves every requirement.** I tried to remove:

- the immutable registration table—it reintroduces cross-window/old-order admission;
- the persisted complete baseline—it makes exact cardinality unprovable to the backend;
- the short table SHARE lock—it leaves the concurrent phantom-order hole;
- owner/runtime artifacts—it turns authority into caller-supplied strings;
- retained custody—it reintroduces the unsafe destructive name-based cleanup;
- the two-table split—it entangles an immutable authority with lifecycle mutation;
- protected-state comparison—it would reduce preservation to trusting old files.

For the blocking finding, the simplest concrete correction is one shared normalized four-UUID protected-fixture predicate used by both `SurgicalResetService` and `TeardownService`, plus an explicitly reviewed synthetic-purchase arming setting. This removes:

- the unprotected second destructive path;
- reliance on a broad route flag without a corresponding destructive-route guard;
- duplicated actor constants;
- the obsolete advisory-lock fiction already removed by R3.

It adds only one narrow shared predicate/module and a small test matrix; it does not add a provider operation, lineage table, or generic reset framework.

## BETTER

A materially better approach is to **decouple synthetic public purchase from destructive E2E mutation-route arming**.

Concretely, use a narrow, runtime-pinned synthetic-purchase enablement flag for the W checkout path, while keeping destructive `/e2e` routes absent unless separately and explicitly armed. Independently retain the shared all-four-UUID refusal for both destructive services as defense in depth.

Comparison:

- **Current design failure modes:** the shared flag is either false, making the required W purchase impossible, or true, importing both destructive routes; one of those routes is not guarded by R3.
- **Narrow-flag approach:** failure is confined to purchase refusal; destructive reset/teardown routes remain structurally absent unless a separate reviewed action arms them. The all-four guard remains as a second barrier.
- **Cost:** one reviewed setting and ASGI/runtime test surface, plus a small shared protected-fixture predicate. It does not alter financial processing, authority registration, or custody semantics.
- **Rollback:** disable the narrow purchase flag or remove the W disjunct; existing paid rights, authority rows, custody, and financial records remain retained.
- **Limit:** because it touches the public checkout guard, it must be an explicit reviewed scope amendment rather than an MP improvisation.
