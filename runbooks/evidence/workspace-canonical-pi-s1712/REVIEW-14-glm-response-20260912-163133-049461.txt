# Answers to the six required questions

## 1. Can old/other-window/P/N/changed-purchase authority gain signed W admission, or can a terminal unregistered refund be fabricated into convergence?

**No, not under the revised design.**

The two-stage design now binds authority to a persisted immutable registration:

- Stage A records the complete fixed seller02/buyer01 order-ID baseline under a short `orders SHARE NOWAIT` transaction.
- Stage B recomputes the complete current fixed-pair set and requires:
  - every baseline ID to remain present;
  - exactly one new-order delta entry;
  - that order to be genuinely paid;
  - unique canonical transaction/Payment/PI/charge/capture bindings;
  - exact actors, amount, and currency;
  - no unregistered refund or ambiguous authority.
- `workspace_refund_test_authorities` uniquely binds window, order, transaction, Payment, PI, and charge, with all columns NOT NULL and UPDATE/DELETE refused by trigger.
- The webhook predicate is after real signature, scope, dedup, and immutable refund-event binding.
- It uses the peer-shaped `admits_workspace_refund(event, db, *, normalized_stripe_account)` interface.
- It locates authority by provider IDs and revalidates all immutable scalar bindings and canonical hashes.
- It locks existing order→transaction→money-state→Payment→Refund rows without calling the inserting `lock_order_money_sync` helper.
- It performs no DML, provider call, or window-table lock.
- A closed/recovery window retains authority only for the original registered charge/order.

A pre-registration refund cannot be converted into authority:

- Stage B rejects an order with an existing unregistered refund.
- The signed pre-registration event is explicitly terminal `completed/e2e_scope_suppressed`, not retryable readiness and not registration.
- The unregistered-refund incident is distinct from the extra-order incident.
- No retroactive bind, extra registration, baseline rewrite, deletion, or automatic closure escape is provided.

P/N cannot satisfy W authority: their actors, persisted authorities, and delivery authority semantics are separate. A changed purchase, scope hash, approval hash, ListingVersion manifest, PI, charge, or pin changes the immutable comparison and refuses. The ListingVersion ambiguity is explicitly tested against `AgentProposal.manifest_hash`, closing the prior model-column shortcut.

Blocking would require a source-backed path where one of those old, cross-window, P/N, changed, or unregistered objects reaches normal W processing or is silently registered later. None was found statically.

## 4. Can activation/recovery or substitution duplicate resources, overwrite generation/Party, or leak a new Workspace secret?

**No within the honestly stated trusted private-operator boundary.**

Custody/provider protection is structurally conservative:

- no DELETE, PATCH, or upsert anywhere in the Workspace custody lifecycle, including recovery;
- authenticated 404 required before first creation;
- durable generation and provider request identity recorded before POST;
- timeout/403/empty parse never treated as absence;
- uncertain creation reconciled against exact scope/name/marker/digest/ID/version;
- changed, deleted/recreated, or unverifiable retained custody refuses;
- future reuse requires exact retained-generation agreement;
- an unseen post-read external writer cannot have its value erased because no destructive operation follows the final GET;
- retained custody ends in `inactive_with_retained_custody` or `deactivation_incomplete`, never false secret-absence success.

Provider account handling requires complete pagination, exact marker uniqueness, no fresh account after expired/uncertain idempotency, and no repair-by-assumption. Foreign Connect ownership is proved and locked before projection; the known party-reassigning upsert is forbidden before that proof. Party/auth_user identity is never deleted.

The host/backend boundary is also honest:

- the authenticated local operator is the trust boundary;
- owner attestation is not misdescribed as cryptographic external verification;
- the backend does not claim to measure Docker from caller strings;
- the host independently discovers source, label, and local image ID;
- OCI index/manifest digests cannot substitute for the local image ID;

- stdin is bounded and strictly parsed;
- `PYTHONPATH=/app` is the sole nonsecret environment exception;
- the canary is scoped to a new synthetic Workspace secret rather than falsely claiming the entire pre-existing application environment is secret-free.

A malicious operator with Docker/custody authority remains explicitly outside the authentication guarantee, which is the correct boundary disclosure.

# SIMPLER

For the cleanup correction, **the simplest concrete alternative is already selected**: one pure normalized fixed-four-UUID helper consumed by both actual services.

Compared with the rejected R2/R3 approaches, this removes:

- the reset/open advisory-lock serialization fiction;
- the strict protected-destructive database branch;
- lineage/mount/owner-pointer dependence from the refusal;
- duplicate per-service identity sets;
- any generic database-trigger policy.

It adds only one small helper, two service call sites, and focused service/ASGI/PostgreSQL tests. It preserves unrelated target behavior and leaves the separately authorized environment lifecycle untouched.

For the overall authority/lifecycle design, **no simpler alternative preserves every invariant**. I tried to remove:

- the immutable registration table — this reopens old/other-window admission;
- the persisted complete baseline — this makes exact one-order cardinality unprovable to the backend;
- `orders SHARE NOWAIT` — this leaves a concurrent phantom-order hole;
- owner/runtime artifacts — this turns authority back into caller-supplied strings;
- retained custody — this reintroduces the unsafe name-based destructive cleanup race;
- the two-table split — this entangles an immutable authority with lifecycle mutation.

Each is load-bearing.

# BETTER

**Advisory only: decouple synthetic public checkout from destructive E2E mutation-route arming.**

A narrower reviewed synthetic-purchase setting could allow the W checkout while leaving `E2E_TEST_ROUTES_ENABLED=false`, so reset/teardown routes remain structurally absent in the final runtime. The shared four-UUID service guard would remain as defense in depth.

- **Failure-mode comparison:**
  - Current R4: one existing flag arms checkout and imports both destructive routes; safety depends on empty allowlists plus service guards, and the existing armed-window monitor needs an explicit disposition.
  - Narrow flag: destructive account routes stay absent by default; checkout failure is isolated. New failure modes are a newly reviewed checkout setting and possible confusion with the existing broad E2E flag.
- **Cost:** one additional setting/router branch and focused checkout/ASGI tests; more scope than R4’s explicit “no new checkout flag” decision.
- **Rollback:** disable the narrow purchase flag or remove the branch; immutable authority, financial rows, custody, and provider history remain unchanged.

This is not a mandate. R4’s choice avoids a new production setting and the required empty-allowlist/service-guard tests materially reduce the imported-route risk. The LOW monitor finding can be resolved either within R4’s selected design or through this narrower future alternative.
