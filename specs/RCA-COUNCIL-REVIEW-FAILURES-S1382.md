## 1. The question

Two of our three Council voters (Kimi, GLM) failed repeatedly on real reviews. Is the cause the models, or the harness we built around them — and if it is the harness, should we keep building it or adopt an existing open-source review harness?

## 3. Root cause

**On this evidence: the harness, not the models' judgment.** The window is narrow (a ~2-day sample); the pattern within it is consistent and mechanically explained, and a confirming sample over subsequent operating days is a stated follow-up rather than a prerequisite for fixing defects we have already root-caused in our own code. We built a bespoke multi-provider *agentic tool-loop* harness — custom turn policing, a coverage controller, byte-token budget projection, and a triple verdict-extraction/salvage stack — and wrapped it around chat-completion models that are weakest exactly at long agentic loops. When the loop and wrapper machinery are taken out of the path (preloaded diff, one structured completion), the same models review competently and cheaply. The failures are self-inflicted: all five classes are defects in code we own — with the caveat that class 1 is partly model behavior (failure to converge in an agentic loop) that our wrapper then failed to force to a terminal verdict; classes 2–5 are wholly ours.

## 4. Are we reinventing the wheel?

Partially, yes. Deterministic diff-and-context packing, schema-validated structured review output, and multi-provider dispatch are exactly what mature open-source PR-review harnesses already do (e.g. Qodo PR-Agent, Apache-2.0). What is genuinely ours and worth keeping: gate semantics, verdict persistence, roster/unanimity rules, and Living State integration. What is commodity and currently defective: context packing, budget projection, and structured-output enforcement.

## 5. Recommendation (for Council review)

1. **(a) Default GLM and Kimi to deterministic context-packing + a single structured completion**, using provider-native `response_format`/`json_schema` (OpenRouter and Moonshot both support it). `GLM_REVIEW_RESPONSE_FORMAT` already exists — **send it to the provider** rather than only validating after the fact. **Precondition (CC R1 M1):** provider-native `json_schema` enforcement is asserted, not yet verified, and OpenRouter enforcement is known to vary by model/routing (some models accept the parameter but fall back to best-effort). The first acceptance criterion of any BQ arising from this RCA is an empirical enforcement probe against the exact model+provider IDs in use (`z-ai/glm-5.2` via OpenRouter; `kimi-k3` via Moonshot): a probe review that intentionally omits and adds a key, with the observed fallback behavior documented. If enforcement proves best-effort only, recommendation (a) still stands for the *packing* half, but schema enforcement remains post-hoc and must then be a single, well-tested validator — not the current triple-extraction stack.
2. **(b) Reserve agentic repo-loop reviews for CC only** (the one reviewer that completes them reliably and cheaply). **Vote-equivalence (CC R1 M2):** this splits the roster into two review kinds, and a single-completion preloaded-diff review is not automatically an equivalent vote to an agentic repo review under the unanimity rule. Proposed resolution, for Council and Max to ratify: a GLM/Kimi single-completion vote counts full-weight **only when** the packed context is the complete, deterministically generated base..head changed-set (verified by the packer, fail-closed if truncated); otherwise the vote is recorded as scope-limited and does not satisfy unanimity on its own. This keeps three full-weight votes the norm while making any depth asymmetry explicit rather than silent. Rollout of (a)/(b) should be reversible and time-boxed: run as a trial with quality acceptance criteria (verdict-agreement with CC and defect-escape rate over the trial window) before either becomes the standing default.
3. **(c) SUPERSEDED by Max decision 2026-07-28 (event d7c3bd3c): ADOPT-FIRST.** The boundary, in Max's words: "Our issue is getting the right files to the LLM and getting the answer back. I believe open source solves that. What we do with it after that is our own code." The commodity layer — context/diff packing, multi-provider dispatch, token/cost accounting, structured answer return — is adopted from open source (candidates: PR-Agent, LiteLLM, provider-native json_schema); keeping any bespoke component there now requires written justification. Everything after the structured answer returns — verdict handling, voting/unanimity, gates, Living State — stays bespoke and owned. The spike question is closed; what remains is dependency selection with version pinning and health checks. — deterministic diff+context packing, schema-validated output, multi-provider — versus continuing to maintain our coverage controller. Outcome is a Council-reviewed BQ with a build-vs-adopt decision on evidence, not preference. The spike's decision criteria must include the **integration-seam cost** of bolting an external harness onto our retained bespoke layer (gate semantics, verdict persistence, roster/unanimity, Living State) and tracking upstream versions — not just commodity-layer feature parity.
4. **(d) Fix the T-2026-000460 mechanics regardless** (coverage-set `base` ignored; Kimi budget cap and byte-as-token reservation): even under (a) these components remain in the path.
5. **(e) Fix track for classes 4–5 (explicit):** recommendation (a) is intended to subsume both — the three coexisting verdict-extraction modes collapse to **one** provider-enforced (or, on best-effort fallback, one well-tested post-hoc) extraction path, and briefs must never request keys beyond the authoritative schema (enforced by the dispatcher, not by convention). If (a) is deferred, classes 4–5 need their own ticket; they must not remain unassigned.

## 6. What this costs us today

Every Kimi/GLM harness failure burns a review round on our single-builder critical path and pushes gates toward CC-only coverage, weakening the independence the unanimity rule exists to provide. The fix direction above makes the two currently unreliable seats reliable again at their observed success cost (observed range roughly $1–4 per review; note CC's completed agentic reviews ran $0.70–$1.52, so the value of the GLM/Kimi seats is reviewer *independence and diversity*, not cost savings over CC) and shrinks the bespoke code we must keep correct.
