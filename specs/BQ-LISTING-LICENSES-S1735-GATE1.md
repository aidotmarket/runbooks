## 0. Max's directive

Sellers attach a licence to their offering. Buyers must accept it before they can purchase. The market offers a small number of stock licences a seller can choose at listing time, plus the option to upload a custom licence. At purchase, if the listing uses an ai.market stock licence the buyer is told it is a balanced document created by the market for both parties; the buyer signs, and we keep the record of the exact licence as signed, downloadable and visible in the buyer's history. If the seller uploaded a custom licence, the buyer is told it is not an ai.market licence and that legal review before signing is their responsibility.

## 1. What exists today (ground truth, Council-verified)

- `listings.license` (free text, migration `20260330_002`); `listings.license_url` and `listings.usage_terms` JSONB (migration `20260915_001`). Never validated, never shown to buyers as terms, never accepted.
- `jsonld_service.LICENSE_URL_MAP` maps five codes: MIT, Apache-2.0, CC-BY-4.0, CC-BY-SA-4.0, CC0-1.0. MIT and Apache are software licences and are wrong for data.
- Gateway v2 publish surface defines `LicenseTerms {terms_ref: SignedReference, commercial_use, retention_days}` on `PublishRequest` — designed, unused, no acceptance record anywhere.
- Purchase path: `POST /checkout/create` → hosted Stripe Checkout → webhook → `FulfillmentService`. No step shows or records terms. Agent/MCP purchases share the path.
- **Platform terms signature already exists and is the template to reuse**: `POST /legal/terms/accept` (`app/api/v1/endpoints/legal_terms.py`) records scope (individual|organization), signer_full_name, signer_title, business_legal_name, authority_ack, three acknowledgement boxes, terms_version, IP and user agent via `record_terms_acceptance`; `GET /legal/terms/acceptance-status` compares against `CURRENT_TERMS_VERSION`. Test buyer-02 accepted terms 1.0 today (reported by Vulcan, S1734 peer message #5807). The licence acceptance is the same shape plus order, listing version, document hash and variant.
- Other reusable patterns: versioned consent record on `data_requests.public_consent_*`; hash-locked signed references and `dataset_commitment_attestations` from the preview work.
- Live business: 33 listings (one a real customer's), 0 purchases. Two real sellers are about to list. Cutover cost is near zero now and grows with every listing.

## 2. Existing licences on the internet — what is usable as stock

### 2a. Open-data licences (free to reuse verbatim; written for free sharing)

| Licence | Steward / year | Fit for a paid B2B marketplace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CDLA-Permissive-2.0 | Linux Foundation, 2021 | One page, plain English, SPDX-listed, written for AI/ML: models and results trained on the data carry no obligation back. Permits redistribution of the data itself, so it only suits sellers who want open distribution. Council (3/3): the right "Open" choice; label it "redistribution allowed". |
| CDLA-Sharing-1.0 | Linux Foundation, 2017 | Share-alike for data; low adoption. Do not offer. |
| CC-BY-4.0 / CC0-1.0 / CC-BY-NC-4.0 | Creative Commons | 4.0 covers database rights; attribution is impractical for trained models; NC is ambiguous for B2B; CC itself advises against using CC to police AI training. Recognise CC-BY-4.0 and CC0 as values on imported listings; do not promote. |
| ODC PDDL / ODC-By / ODbL | Open Knowledge Foundation | Older, database-oriented; ODbL is copyleft. Recognise, do not promote. |
| O-UDA / C-UDA | Microsoft, 2019 | C-UDA limits use to computational use (analysis/ML) and forbids redistributing the data while leaving results free — the open licence closest to what a paying buyer wants, but low adoption and awkward to explain. Borrow the concept, not the text. |
| Montreal Data License | 2019 | A taxonomy of ML-use restrictions, not an adopted licence. Useful only as a checklist of parameters. |

None is suitable as the default for a paid listing: each either permits redistribution of the raw data (destroying the value the seller is charging for) or is unfamiliar to buyers.

### 2b. What paid data marketplaces actually do (the model to copy)

- AWS Data Exchange: one standard Data Subscription Agreement (DSA), stated to be "developed in collaboration with the buyer and seller community" and "well-balanced". Verified from the published text: non-exclusive, worldwide, non-transferable licence to use, modify and create Derived Data internally; no redistribution, resale, or re-identification of individuals; provider warrants lawful sourcing and no regulated personal data; no accuracy warranty, remedy fix-or-refund within 30 days; aggregate liability capped at "the greater of three times the subscriber spend in the twelve months preceding the event … or $1 million" (§6.2); provider indemnities for rights and unlawful data; data removed within 90 days of termination; New York law (§10.1); "Neither Amazon Web Services, Inc. nor any of its Affiliates are a party to this Agreement" (§10.3). Providers may use the DSA, the DSA plus pre-drafted addenda, or a custom EULA; buyers accept by click-through at subscription and the terms are frozen for the life of the subscription.
- Snowflake Marketplace: a Standard Marketplace Agreement or the provider's custom/offline terms, chosen per listing in a Legal Terms section; the consumer's authorised administrator accepts at get time.
- EU Data Act Model Contractual Terms (European Commission, published 19 Nov 2025 as non-binding guidance; free): four templates including Data Sharer → Data Recipient for voluntary B2B data sharing, drafted to respect the Data Act's unfair-terms chapter (Art. 13). The only free, official B2B data-provision template; we borrow its balance and clause list, not its law (the Standard is under US law, §7.3).

We mirror the AWS clause structure and balance, not its words; the EU MCT text is free to adapt. Historical note, non-normative: at consultation DeepSeek cautioned that a cap "at fees paid" can be voidable in some EU B2B contexts; under US law with no lawyer review (§7.4) the Standard adopts the AWS-style cap (a multiple of fees, same on both sides).

## 3. Recommendation (v2)

**Launch with two choices, add the third on demand.**

1. **ai.market Standard Data Licence** (default, recommended). A short (2–3 page) agreement drafted by us from the EU MCT Data Sharer → Data Recipient template using the DSA clause list, under the governing law fixed in the draft (§7.3: USA; New York proposed). No lawyer review (§7.4); the Council reviews the text at Gate 2 as it reviews code. Substance: seller is the licensor; buyer receives a perpetual, non-exclusive, non-transferable licence to the delivered dataset version for internal business use by the buying entity, its affiliates and contractors acting for it; analysis, building products and services; models, outputs and derived works belong to the buyer and may be commercialised provided the raw dataset cannot be extracted or reconstructed from them; no redistribution or resale of the data itself; no re-identification; free samples and previews are evaluation-only; seller warrants rights and lawful sourcing and that the data contains no regulated personal or special-category data (v1: prohibited outright — no "declared" path, no DPA); no accuracy warranty beyond the listing's stated facts; 7-day inspection window then acceptance is final; remedy fix-or-refund; liability capped on both sides including the seller's rights warranty at three times fees paid (AWS DSA precedent; uncapped seller IP indemnity would make small sellers refuse to list); licence ends on refund with a buyer deletion obligation; ai.market is not a party. **One in-document switch: AI/ML training permitted yes/no.** The switch is a hashed document variant — `license_sha256 = H(canonical(text) ‖ canonical(params))` — never a second document. Default **yes** (Max decision 2); the seller may switch it off; the setting is shown conspicuously.
2. **Seller's own licence** (custom upload). PDF or plain text, size-capped, scanned, hashed, displayed verbatim, take-it-or-leave-it (P5: no buyer–seller negotiation). Amber notice to the buyer. Marketplace terms prevail on non-custodial delivery, commission, refund, dispute-hold and the evaluation-only sample rule. **AI training on a custom listing** is never inferred from the seller's PDF: it is a separately hashed one-paragraph *marketplace AI-training rider* (default yes, seller may opt out at submission) that is part of the seller's submission and of the buyer's record, so the buyer relies on an instrument the seller actually accepted.
3. **Open — CDLA-Permissive-2.0 verbatim ("redistribution allowed")**: not a launch card. Add when a seller asks; it is verbatim text and a one-hour change. (GLM: removes a card, a seed document and open-redistribution UX from launch; DeepSeek and Gemini endorse CDLA when offered.)

**Marketplace listing covenant (all listings, any licence).** The seller's rights/lawful-sourcing/no-personal-data warranties and the evaluation-only sample rule live in a short ai.market covenant the seller accepts at listing submission — separate from the licence text. This is what makes Open and custom listings safe: CDLA is "AS IS" and carries no title warranty, and a custom licence may be silent on samples. The buyer's record shows the licence plus this covenant.

Recognised legacy values (no card, no summary of ours): CC-BY-4.0, CC0-1.0. Drop MIT and Apache-2.0 from the JSON-LD map.

Every stock document is versioned and hashed with two parts: a **summary** (five to seven plain-English lines, labelled "not the contract") and the **full text**, both visible and downloadable on the listing page before purchase, not only at the Buy screen.

## 4. Simplest possible flows

**Seller, at listing submission (one screen):** "How can buyers use this data?" — two cards: Standard (recommended; toggle "Allow AI/ML training" pre-set to Allow, conspicuously shown), My own licence (upload; the AI-training rider toggle, pre-set to Allow). One confirmation toggle accepts the marketplace covenant. That is the seller's signature event: user, organisation, business legal name and jurisdiction (typed, as the platform terms flow already asks), time, IP, licence hash and covenant code/version/hash. AIM Data, Seller Workspace and API publish paths all send the same fields; a route that omits the training switch gets the default (yes); a publish without a covenant acceptance fails closed.

**Buyer, at Buy (one screen before Stripe):** summary; green badge "ai.market standard terms — the same balanced terms every seller on ai.market uses. ai.market is not a party and gives no legal advice" or amber badge "The seller's own terms. ai.market did not write these; review them before you accept"; full text expandable and downloadable; buyer types full name, ticks "I am authorised to accept for <organisation>" and clicks Accept and continue to payment. That is the signature: typed name + click + identity, organisation, time, IP, user agent, document hash, variant. Council (3/3): sufficient for B2B under US E-SIGN/UETA and eIDAS simple e-signature; initials and drawn signatures add friction, not enforceability.

**Atomic with order creation, at the one constructor.** `OrderService.create_order` (`app/services/order_service.py:638`) becomes the only way an order comes into existence and requires the acceptance fields (`accept_license_sha256`, `accept_covenant_sha256`, `typed_name`, `authority_confirmed`, principal); it writes the `license_acceptances` row and the licence-bearing `listing_snapshot` in the same transaction, and refuses (no order, no wallet debit) on a missing or stale hash or absent authority. All five current call sites route through it unchanged in role: `checkout.py:224`, `mcp_marketplace.py:670`, `app/services/mcp_marketplace_tools.py:816`, `transaction_service.py:880` (agent checkout), `transaction_service.py:1319` (fixed-price auto-accept); `AgentService`'s direct `INSERT INTO orders` (`agent_service.py:544`, wallet purchase, bypasses Stripe) is removed and routed through the constructor. The Stripe webhook re-verifies the hash before fulfilment. Abandoned checkouts leave an order in its normal abandoned state with its acceptance attached — no orphan acceptance can exist.

**After payment:** the signed licence record (licence full text + covenant + rider + acceptance block: parties' legal names and jurisdictions, organisation, listing, dataset version, order, time, hashes) is rendered on demand from the database row — the row and its hashes are the source of truth, nothing is stored as PDF (DeepSeek SIMPLER) — downloadable from the buyer's order history and the seller's sales history, and linked in the order email. Refund or dispute-hold outcome marks it terminated with a deletion obligation, through one audited status transition.

**Agents (P3/S8):** listing JSON-LD carries `license` as a canonical URL `https://ai.market/licenses/<code>/<version>[/<variant>]`; the agent purchase API exposes summary, full-text URL and hash; order creation requires `accept_license_sha256` and `authority_confirmed: true`, and the record stores the principal (the user/org that authorised the API credential) and the credential id, with `typed_name` null for `channel in (api, agent)`. A hash alone proves possession of a string, not authority; the principal binding is what makes agent purchases enforceable. Gate 2 may choose GLM's stronger form — a short-lived single-use signed acceptance-intent carrying principal, listing version, hashes, variant, nonce and expiry, verified at the constructor — in place of the bare hash plus authenticated principal; the record is the same either way.

## 4a. Enforcement at every delivery door (absorbed from S1720; corrected at Gate 1 r1)

The acceptance is only worth something if no data can leave without it. Two shared chokepoints, both fail-closed:

1. **Creation:** `OrderService.create_order` (§4) — no order without a 1:1 active acceptance.
2. **Issuance:** one order-locked helper `require_active_license_acceptance(order_id)` that refuses (403/409, no URL, token, credential, redirect or bytes) when `license_acceptances.status != active`, called at the moment anything is issued, not only at first dispatch. The r1 review established that today's doors do **not** all pass through `OrderService._authorize_download_access` (`order_service.py:404`), so the helper is required at each of these issuance points: `OrderService._authorize_download_access` and `_assert_current_access` (`:379`; used by `raw_download_service.generate_download_token` `:45/:90` behind `POST /orders/{id}/download-token` and the reference-delivery `GET /orders/{id}/access`); `DeliveryService.generate_delivery_token` (`:133`), `validate_token` (`:234`) and `stream_from_vz` (`:286`) behind `POST /deliveries/{tx}/token`, `GET /deliveries/{tx}/download-url`, `GET /deliveries/{tx}/download` (these check transaction ownership via `_assert_buyer_access` `:1438` and nothing else); `FulfillmentService.request_fulfillment` (`:141`) **and** `handle_fulfillment_response` (`:304`, the asynchronous Trust-Channel reply that persists credentials at `:426-452` — must recheck, a termination can land between dispatch and reply); `POST /orders/{id}/delivery/refresh`; Seller Workspace `prepare → download → per-file grant` (`seller_workspace_delivery.py:82-100`, already on the authoriser); AIM Data manifest delivery `delivery_buyer.py:34-41`, `POST /orders/{id}/members/grant`, `GET /orders/{id}/members/{index}`, `GET /orders/{id}/download-file` (`consume_download_token`); the agent artifact path `fulfillment_broker.py:133`. The legacy internal-key `POST /generate-download-token` (`app/api/v1/endpoints/jwt.py:14`) mints a download JWT from listing/user with no order at all: Gate 2 deprecates it or routes it through the helper. `bq-buyer-download-unification-s1711` collapses the door count later; this item does not wait for it. Gate 2 acceptance test: with an acceptance missing, and separately terminated, every route above returns 403/409 and discloses nothing; concurrent termination versus issuance loses to the order lock.

The licence of record is also frozen into `orders.listing_snapshot` (NOT NULL, `app/models/order.py:132`) so the order carries the exact text and hashes without a join.

S1720's open product questions, answered by today's decisions: acceptance is before payment, so there is no pay-then-decline refund path; the licence is per listing, with the Standard reusable by construction and a seller's custom document reusable across that seller's listings; a purchase's acceptance carries across later versions delivered under the same purchase and is asked again on a new purchase.

## 5. Data model (v4, minimal)

- Stock instruments live in the backend repository (YAML/markdown with frontmatter), seeded and hashed at deploy: the Standard licence (with the `ai_training` variant), the marketplace covenant, and the AI-training rider. `license_documents` holds only custom uploads: id, owner_seller_id, title, full_text/pdf_ref, sha256, status, created_at.
- On `listings` (captured onto `listing_versions` at publish so each delivered version pins its terms): `license_code`, `license_version`, `license_params` JSONB (`{"ai_training": true}`), `license_document_id` (custom only), `license_sha256` (= H(text ‖ params)), `rider_sha256` (custom only), `covenant_code/version/sha256`, `seller_accepted_at`, `seller_user_id`, `seller_org_id`, `seller_legal_name`, `seller_jurisdiction`, `seller_acceptance_source` (`submission` | `platform_terms_v<N>` for inherited listings, §6.12). Publish fails closed without covenant acceptance and seller identity. No history table in v1: immutable acceptances pin every past purchase; a licence change updates the pointer for future sales only.
- `license_acceptances` (child of `orders`, 1:1, written only by `OrderService.create_order`): order_id, listing_id, listing_version_id, license_code/version/params or document_id, license_sha256, rider_sha256, covenant_sha256, buyer_user_id, buyer_org_id, buyer_legal_name, buyer_jurisdiction, seller_org_id, seller_legal_name, seller_jurisdiction, typed_name (nullable for api/agent), signer_title, authority_confirmed, principal_ref, credential_id, accepted_at, ip, user_agent, channel, status (active|terminated), terminated_at, terminated_reason. Contract fields are immutable; `status` is the only mutable column and changes through one audited transition that also writes an Event Ledger row.
- **Legal identity provenance** (r1): `organizations` has only `name` today (`app/models/organization.py:24-34`); legal identity exists only on `finance.BillingEntity` (`app/models/finance.py:37-44`). Gate 2 adds `legal_name` and `jurisdiction` to `organizations`, filled from the seller's Stripe Connect billing entity where present and otherwise from the `business_legal_name` the platform terms flow already collects, plus a jurisdiction field added to that flow; first acceptance on either side back-fills them.
- **Reuse of the `/legal/terms` flow is shape-only** (r1): `TermsAcceptanceRequest`'s fields and `record_terms_acceptance`'s IP/user-agent/non-blank validation are reused; the function itself is not called from order creation because it hardcodes the platform terms, commits internally (`terms_acceptance_service.py:203-204`) and its table is append-only (`20260707_002`). Gate 2 extracts a transaction-neutral recorder (`record_license_acceptance(session, …)`, no commit) used inside the constructor's transaction.
- Fold the unused gateway `LicenseTerms` into this shape; the hash-locked signed-reference pattern from `dataset_commitment_attestations` (`app/models/listing_enrichment.py:140`) makes the record verifiable without trusting any rendering.

## 6. Gaps Max asked about (v2, Council-augmented)

1. **Version pinning.** Record binds the exact document hash and variant at acceptance; seller changes apply to future sales only; later dataset versions delivered under the same purchase keep the original terms.
2. **Who is the licensor.** The seller. ai.market drafts the Standard and is not a party; the "created by the market, balanced for both" line is a description, never a warranty, with "not a party, no legal advice" alongside.
3. **Both parties' legal identity.** The record must carry the seller's legal entity and jurisdiction and the buyer's; without them the record is not enforceable. Source and back-fill are defined in §5; a seller cannot publish without them.
4. **Seller-side acceptance.** Via the marketplace covenant at submission (§3); one toggle, recorded like a signature.
5. **Samples and previews.** Evaluation-only under the covenant, regardless of licence; a licence choice is required before any sample is servable.
6. **Personal data.** v1 prohibits regulated personal and special-category data outright. A "declared personal data + DPA" path is a later phase; a declared listing with no governing terms is worse than no listing.
7. **Refunds and disputes.** Refund = terminated licence + buyer deletion obligation (buyer confirms deletion in the order history). Ties to `bq-refund-safe-production-s1714` and `bq-dispute-hold-gate-s1711`. Non-refund termination (breach) is by the seller's notice through ai.market, not direct contact.
8. **Inspection window.** Non-custodial data cannot be un-downloaded; 7 days for defects, then final.
9. **Governing law.** USA (Max, §7.3); the draft names New York, following the AWS DSA precedent.
10. **Business-only.** The buyer confirms business use and authority for a named organisation (the platform terms flow already does this); no lawyer conclusion is sought (§7.4).
11. **Record custody and retention.** Signed records are contracts, not customer data; storing them is inside the non-custodial boundary. Legal-hold exemption in `account-teardown.md` for the statutory limitation period; retrievable by either party after account closure.
12. **Existing listings** (33 today, one of them a real customer's). No cutover rule (§7.6): they default to Standard with AI training allowed and no per-listing prompt. The seller-side authority for that default is the platform terms: Gate 2 bumps `CURRENT_TERMS_VERSION` with one sentence ("listings without a chosen licence are offered under the ai.market Standard Data Licence with AI training permitted, and the seller accepts the marketplace covenant for them"), which every seller accepts through the existing `require_terms_acceptance` gate at their next gated action (publish or order); inherited listings record `seller_acceptance_source = platform_terms_v<N>` and become purchasable once their seller has accepted that version. Nothing fabricates a `seller_accepted_at`. Recognised free-text `listings.license` values are mapped; the rest fall under the default.
13. **Custom-licence hygiene.** Text/PDF only, size cap, malware scan, immutable hash, verbatim display; English canonical, translations informational only (P10); prohibited-terms check (nothing contradicting non-custodial delivery, commission, refund, dispute rules).
14. **Export, sanctions, seller notification.** Seller is notified of each acceptance; sanctions screening is already in the money path — confirm the licence record links to it.
15. **Tax.** A data licence is an electronically supplied service for VAT; confirm the Stripe Tax product code. Configuration, not design.

## 7. Max's decisions (2026-09-21, S1735)

1. Launch scope: Standard + seller's own licence. Open (CDLA-Permissive-2.0) added on first seller request. **Decided.**
2. AI-training switch: **yes is the default** ("we are a market for AI data"). The seller can turn it off; the setting is still shown conspicuously and pinned into the hash. Supersedes §3/§4 "no silent default" — publish routes that omit the value get `ai_training: true`.
3. Governing law: **USA.** State to be fixed in the draft; New York proposed, following the AWS DSA precedent. Update §6.9.
4. Lawyer review: **not needed** (Max). The Standard licence and covenant ship on our own drafting; §6.10's written B2B-eligibility conclusion is dropped.
5. Personal data: **prohibited outright in v1.** Decided.
6. Existing listings: **no cutover rule.** One real customer listing exists; existing listings default to Standard with AI training allowed and no seller prompt is required. Supersedes §6.12.

## 8. Out of scope

Negotiated or private offers; per-buyer pricing tied to licence; enforcement technology (watermarking); subscription/refresh licensing beyond the pinned-version rule; contract text in languages other than English; DPA path for personal data.

## 9. Gate 2 mandates (binding; from the Gate 1 r2 verdicts)

Each mandate names its test. Gate 3 checks every one.

- M1 **Single constructor.** Remove `AgentService`'s `INSERT INTO orders` (`app/services/agent_service.py:544`); wallet purchase goes through `OrderService.create_order`. Test: `grep -rn "INSERT INTO orders"` finds only `order_service.py`; the wallet-purchase E2E yields exactly one order and one 1:1 acceptance.
- M2 **Refuse at creation.** `create_order` rejects a missing or stale `accept_license_sha256`, `accept_covenant_sha256`, and for custom listings `accept_rider_sha256` (or one domain-separated composite hash covering licence text + params + rider + covenant), `authority_confirmed:false`, or missing buyer legal identity after the M6 back-fill — no order, no wallet debit, no commit. Test: unit tests raise per condition; a forced recorder failure rolls back order and acceptance together; changing the rider after display invalidates the request.
- M3 **Transaction-neutral recorder.** `record_license_acceptance(session, …)` with no commit, used inside the constructor's transaction; `record_terms_acceptance` is never called from order creation. Test: forced Stripe/DB failure leaves no orphan row.
- M4 **Issuance helper at every door.** `require_active_license_acceptance(order_id)` fails closed (403/409, no URL, token, credential, redirect or bytes) when `status != active`, at every §4a point including the asynchronous `handle_fulfillment_response` recheck and the agent routes `GET /agent/orders/{id}/data` (`app/api/v1/agent/router.py:878-917`) and `GET /agent/orders/{id}/artifact` (`:1120-1164`, via `fulfillment_broker.py:171-246` and `order_service.py:1228-1263`). Test: parameterised over a route inventory **generated from the FastAPI route table** (DeepSeek BETTER), with missing and with terminated acceptance; concurrent termination versus issuance loses to the order lock.
- M5 **Legacy door.** `POST /generate-download-token` (`app/api/v1/endpoints/jwt.py:14`) is removed or bound to an order and the helper. Test: no order-less download JWT reaches delivery.
- M6 **Legal identity.** Add `organizations.legal_name` and `jurisdiction`; back-fill from `BillingEntity` (`legal_name`, `country_code`), else the typed platform-terms `business_legal_name`; add jurisdiction to the `/legal/terms` flow. Gate 2 defines the allowed party types (organisation, or a sole trader / legal individual with typed legal name and jurisdiction), the source precedence (Stripe billing entity over typed, typed over nothing), and the conflict response (409 with both values, reconcilable by the party). Publish fails closed without seller identity; order creation fails closed without buyer identity. Test: missing or conflicting identity is rejected; back-fill observed; the acceptance pins the final names and jurisdictions.
- M7 **Hashing.** Byte-exact canonical form shared by backend, seeded documents, frontend and API clients: canonical JSON (sorted keys, UTF-8, NFC), domain-separated and length-prefixed construction for licence text, params, rider and covenant; unknown or malformed params fail closed. Test: published test vectors reproduce identical hashes across implementations; flipping `ai_training` changes the hash; a custom listing that omits the rider defaults to rider `true`.
- M8 **Seller acceptance record.** Each listing/version references a non-null immutable `seller_acceptance_id` (an append-only row carrying signer name, title, authority, IP, user agent, hashes), or the inherited-listing provenance `platform_terms_v<N>` plus the seller's terms-acceptance row id. Purchase performs its own seller-current-version check, independent of `TERMS_GATE_MODE=shadow` (`app/core/config.py:154-160`, `terms_acceptance_service.py:273-278`); an inherited listing whose seller has not accepted the bumped version fails closed with 409 `SELLER_TERMS_ACCEPTANCE_PENDING`. Never fabricate `seller_accepted_at`. Test: publish/purchase fails before seller acceptance even in shadow mode; after acceptance the listing pins the exact row; a covenant change affects only future acceptances.
- M9 **Status transition.** `license_acceptances.status` is the only mutable column; `active → terminated` through one audited transition that writes an Event Ledger row; refund and dispute outcomes terminate with a buyer deletion obligation. Test: mutation of contract fields is blocked; termination writes the audit row.
- M10 **x402.** The feature-flagged x402 path (`app/api/v1/endpoints/x402.py:50-149`, `app/services/x402_service.py:120-324`, `X402_ENABLED` default false at `app/core/config.py:653-655`) creates a paid transaction and an access token with no order or acceptance. Gate 2 either hard-disables it for this launch with a test proving the route and listing flag fail closed, or routes it through a validated acceptance-intent and the constructor before any access token. Test: with x402 enabled, a missing or stale hash produces no transaction, payment, order, acceptance or token.
- M11 **Custom-upload hygiene** (§6.13): text/PDF only, size cap, malware scan, secret-pattern rejection, immutable hash, verbatim display, prohibited-terms check; sanctions screening linked from the record.
- M12 **Gate 2 spec contents.** The Standard licence text (with the `ai_training` variant), the marketplace covenant, the AI-training rider, the buyer and seller summaries, the JSON-LD `license` URL scheme replacing `LICENSE_URL_MAP`, the agent purchase API fields, the AIM Data / Seller Workspace publish-field changes, and the platform-terms sentence for §6.12 — all reviewed by the Council as text, since there is no lawyer review (§7.4).
