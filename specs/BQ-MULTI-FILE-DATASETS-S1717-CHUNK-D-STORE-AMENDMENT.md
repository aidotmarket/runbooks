## 1. Why D5 must be restated

Gate 1 D5 stages every member of a directory order under `STAGING_DIR/orders/<order_id>/` with fsync/rename semantics and serves member fetches from that directory. Gate 2 §2/§4.4 record that `STAGING_DIR` is `/tmp/fulfillment` on a Railway container with **no volume**: every in-flight and delivered order would be lost on the next deploy. Max chose an ai.market-owned Cloudflare R2 bucket over a Railway volume (D-G2-1). Object storage has no directories, no partial files, no rename and no fsync; multipart uploads have minimum part sizes; the buyer can be handed a presigned URL instead of a stream. So the *mechanics* of D5 — receive, verify, finalize, sweep, serve — must be restated against the store. The *product rules* of D5 do not change: the whole set is delivered under the purchased version's manifest; nothing is `delivered` until every member matches; the device never selects the mode; resume is server-authoritative; one open session per order; immutability from `delivered`; entitlement per set.

## 3. D5 restated against the store (normative where it differs from Gate 1)

### 3.1 Store client and placement
Chunk D extends chunk C0's `app/services/listing_asset_store.py` (the one bounded client for `LISTING_ASSET_BUCKET`, Gate 2 §4.2a) with `create_multipart`, `upload_part`, `complete_multipart`, `abort_multipart`, `list_multipart_uploads(prefix)`, `head`, `get_stream`, `delete_prefix` and `presign_get(key, ttl, response_content_disposition)`; no other module talks to the bucket. boto3 is synchronous: every store call runs in the listener's thread pool (`asyncio.to_thread`). **Three bounded executors, never shared:** (1) the **chunk-path executor** with one reserved worker per admitted session (`TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS` workers; admission in §3.2(a) is what bounds it), used only by `handle_chunk`'s store calls; (2) the **finalizer executor**, `TRANSFER_FINALIZER_WORKERS` = 8 per replica, on which the per-session finalizers (§3.4) and completion jobs (§3.5) queue; (3) the **sweep executor** (16 workers) for HEAD sweeps and the scheduler's sweeps. On the **chunk path** every synchronous store call made for the chunks of one **ACK window** — the ≤ 4 chunks the sender is waiting on, which may flush up to four one-chunk members (`put_object` each) or one `create_multipart` + `upload_part` — runs under **one wall-clock deadline per window**, `TRANSFER_CHUNK_PATH_DEADLINE_S` = 20 s, **measured from the first un-acked chunk's arrival and including executor queue time** (per-call 5 s connect / 15 s read, **no retry**); it must stay inside the shipped sender's 30 s ACK timeout (`aim-data:app/services/fulfillment_service.py:57`, `:457-488`), because the response to the chunk that fills a part is returned only after the part commits (§3.3). Exceeding it is a session-level error. **Nothing else runs on that path:** `CompleteMultipartUpload`, the finalization re-hash read, reconciliation (b) and the completion sweep run on the other executors, never inside `handle_chunk`. Elsewhere the budget is `TRANSFER_STORE_TIMEOUT_S` (10 s / 60 s, one retry), except the buyer's issuance HEAD (§3.6), which uses the chunk-path budget (5/15 s, no retry) so its bound is known. **Store error classes:** `NoSuchUpload`/`NoSuchPart`/`InvalidPart` (on `UploadPart`/`CompleteMultipartUpload`/`ListParts`) and `NoSuchKey`/`404` on a key the listener expects (final key of a `verified_at` member) are **member resets** (§3.4 repair: chunk and part receipts deleted, counters and `upload_id` cleared, member reported unverified) — they mean the store no longer holds what the receipts claim; every other store failure (timeout, 5xx, auth) is a **session-level** error (the order stays resumable; `vai.fulfillment.error` semantics of D5 unchanged). Credentials are the four Infisical-managed variables of `listing-asset-store.md`; the token is bucket-scoped Object Read & Write, so a compromised backend can reach only this bucket. Key prefixes owned by chunk D: `orders/` only; chunk D's sweeps never read or delete outside it (the same prefix-ownership rule as the workspace amendment W-D3 (iii)).

### 3.2 Session start (metadata)
As D5, with two additions. (a) **Admission bound:** the listener holds at most `TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS` open manifest-mode sessions per backend replica (one in-memory part buffer each); a metadata message beyond that is refused before session creation with `listener_busy`, the order stays undelivered and the device retries after `TRANSFER_RETRY_AFTER_S` — a refusal payload value, not a new message. (b) **Resume reconciliation from the store, under the per-order lock** (everywhere in this document "the per-order lock" is the shipped `orders`-row `SELECT … FOR UPDATE OF o` of `_lock_owned_order` — defined at `fulfillment_listener_service.py:567`, the lock at `:579`, called at `:312` (complete) and `:493` (error), and taken inline in `handle_metadata` at `:138`; the `TransferSession` `.with_for_update()` at `:213`/`:320`/`:498`/`:651` is the *session-row* lock, a different lock that stays as shipped. **In manifest mode `handle_chunk` and the grant path newly acquire the orders-row lock** — `handle_chunk` takes it **before** the session-row lock, the shipped order "order precedes transfer on every path that acquires both" (`:491-492`) — scoped to the staging-state read plus the receipt (or meter) transaction and never held across a store call; a DB row lock, so the out-of-process sweeps of §3.7 take exactly the same lock)**. Continuation vs resume:** a metadata message for an order whose session is still `receiving` (the device reconnected after a websocket drop or a backend replica restart) is D5's idempotent replay and **continues** that session — its `transfer_id`, receipts and `upload_id`s are intact, the HEAD/`upload_id` checks below run, and streaming carries on; a **resume** is a new session created only when the previous one is terminal (`TIMED_OUT`, `FAILED`, or none), and D5's session-row sweep then deletes the old session's unverified receipts (chunk and part) and aborts their open uploads (they carry no live `upload_id` any more), while verified objects are retained**:** the order's staging state must be `active` (§3.7; `purging` → refused with `listener_busy`, `purged` → a fresh generation is minted, every member reset, and the session starts empty); for every member with `verified_at`, `HEAD` on its final key must return the recorded ETag and size, else the member is reset (D5 (c), now O(verified members) HEAD calls at session start — 21,000 Class B operations at the trigger scale, ≈ $0.008); for every unverified member with part receipts (continuation only — a resume has none), its persisted `upload_id` must appear in `list_multipart_uploads(prefix)` (fetched once per generation prefix, paginated, membership checked in memory; else the member is reset), and every open upload under the generation prefix that is not a persisted `upload_id` is aborted (**invariant: at most one open multipart upload per member key**, adopted by persisted id, never by key alone). **The orphan test is evaluated against durable state only** — the DB rows, never the replica's in-memory registry — so a replica restart that loses the part buffer never aborts an upload whose parts are receipted: the buffer is gone, the `upload_id` and part receipts are not, and the next part uploads under the same id. `verified_member_indices` is computed only from members that passed the HEAD.

### 3.3 Chunk arrival: part window
The frozen chunk plan (D5) additionally groups each member's chunks into **parts** of `TRANSFER_PART_BYTES` (8 MiB = 128 chunks of 65,536 bytes; the last part of a member is shorter; a member of ≤ `TRANSFER_PART_BYTES` is one part uploaded by `PutObject`). Per open session the listener keeps **one in-memory part buffer** with a per-chunk bitmap. Order of operations per chunk: validate `chunk_index`/`member_index`/`byte_offset`/length against the plan → verify `chunk_sha256` → look up the chunk receipt: present and equal → acknowledged, nothing written; present and different → refused; absent and the chunk belongs to the **current part** (the lowest part of the lowest unverified member with no part receipt, or the part currently buffered) → copied into the buffer at its offset (a resend within the buffer overwrites identical bytes); absent and the chunk belongs to a **later** part → refused with `out_of_window` (session stays open; the device re-sends metadata and resumes, §2). **Deliberate deviation from D5, stated for the vote:** D5 tolerates arbitrary out-of-order arrival; this amendment tolerates it only within the current part, because a multipart part must be complete before it can be written. The plan-order sender never triggers `out_of_window` except after a member reset (below). **ACK cadence:** the listener acknowledges on the shipped `ACK_WINDOW` (every 4 *global* chunk indices, `fulfillment_listener_service.py:276-284`) as soon as the chunks are accepted into the buffer — an ACK is backpressure, not durability. Part boundaries are **not** in general window-aligned (a member's parts start at its own first index), so the rule is stated without alignment: **the response to the chunk that fills a part is returned after that part commits**, because the shipped serial handler (`app/api/v1/endpoints/trust_websocket.py:776-777`, `:1282`) returns `handle_chunk`'s result; whether that response is an ACK or a plain per-chunk result, the sender's next ACK wait cannot be satisfied before it. This is why the chunk-path deadline (§3.1) is bounded inside the sender's 30 s ACK timeout; a part commit that exceeds it answers the chunk with a session-level error, the shipped sender fails the session, and the order resumes by a new session (verified members retained by the §3.2 HEAD check; that session's unverified receipts swept and re-streamed). **Refusals are immediate:** a refusal (`out_of_window`, `member_reset`, plan violation, session-level error) is the chunk's own response regardless of window position — the shipped listener already answers its per-chunk refusals that way (`fulfillment_listener_service.py:217`/`:224`/`:228`/`:239`/`:244`) — and the chunk D sender consumes it whenever it arrives (§2). **Member reset during an open session:** when a member is reset (§3.1 error class, §3.4 mismatch, lease), the listener answers the device's next chunk for that member with `member_reset`; the device re-sends metadata and resumes (§2). When the buffer's bitmap is full, the listener calls `UploadPart` (creating the multipart upload on the member's first part and persisting its `upload_id`; or `PutObject` for a single-part member), and on success inserts, **in one transaction**, the part receipt with the returned ETag and the part's chunk receipts, advancing `bytes_received` and `chunks_received` on `transfer_session_members`. **A chunk receipt therefore still implies durable bytes** (in the store, under an open multipart upload or a completed object) — stated precisely as a **reconcile-before-use invariant**, not cross-store atomicity: a receipt records an observed successful store write; an out-of-band loss afterwards (a lifecycle or manual abort, a deleted object) can leave receipts pointing at nothing until the next use, and every use of receipts is preceded by a store check that resets the member on loss — resume (§3.2(b): `upload_id` in `list_multipart_uploads`, HEAD on verified keys), finalization (§3.4: `NoSuchUpload`/`NoSuchPart` on `CompleteMultipartUpload`), completion (§3.5: HEAD), and the listener's own aborts (§3.7, lease), which delete the member's receipts in the same step. Receipts are therefore never *used* for `verified_member_indices`, finalization or delivery without a store check that would expose the loss; with a dead scheduler and a silent sender the stale receipts simply wait for the next message or resume, where they are reset. A crash before `UploadPart` returns loses only the buffer: no receipts exist for those chunks; on the continuation (metadata replay, §3.2) the member is reported unverified and the device re-streams it from its first chunk; chunks of already-receipted parts are acknowledged idempotently without any store write, so a re-stream costs wire but not store. Per-chunk work is O(1) (one bitmap set and one memcpy; one indexed receipt lookup) and per-part work is one store call and one transaction of ≤ 129 rows. Zero-byte member: one zero-length `PutObject` (matches the shipped sender's single empty chunk).

### 3.4 Member finalization
**Finalization runs off the ACK critical path:** each open session owns one background finalizer task (same replica, same process as the websocket handler; its store calls run on the finalizer executor, §3.1, so they can never occupy a chunk-path worker); when a member's part receipts equal its expected part count, `handle_chunk` only enqueues the member and returns. The finalizer, under the per-order lock for its row writes only, calls `CompleteMultipartUpload` with the recorded ETags in order (or the `PutObject` already completed it), then **streams the object back from the store and hashes it** (budget `TRANSFER_STORE_TIMEOUT_S`, one retry; a 2 GiB read takes minutes and never delays an ACK); equal to the manifest `sha256` → `verified_at`, the object's ETag and size are recorded on `transfer_session_members`; different → `DeleteObject`, receipts and part receipts for that member deleted, counters reset, member reported unverified (D5's repair rule; the device streams it again). The store read is the equivalent of D5's "hash from wherever the bytes are": it verifies what the store holds, not what the listener sent. `TRANSFER_MAX_MEMBER_BYTES` (2 GiB) → at most 256 parts per member, well under R2's 10,000. Reconciliation (b) of D5 becomes: any member whose part receipts are complete and whose `verified_at` is null is enqueued to the finalizer on the next message for the session (bounded to that message's member plus members left in that state by a crash) — the message itself is answered at once; a crash between `CompleteMultipartUpload` and `verified_at` is repaired by a `HEAD` (object present → re-hash and record; absent → reset). **Complete vs finalization:** see §3.5 — `vai.fulfillment.complete` never blocks on finalization or on the completion sweep; it answers within `TRANSFER_FINALIZE_WAIT_S` either with the grant or with `finalizing`. **Absolute upload lease:** a member's open upload older than `TRANSFER_UPLOAD_MAX_AGE_S` (3 days from `create_multipart`) is aborted by the listener on the next message or sweep and the member reset — so a live-but-slow transfer is re-streamed member by member rather than ever meeting the 8-day lifecycle rule (§3.7), and D5's 6-hour idle TTL keeps its meaning.

### 3.5 Completion transaction
**The completion job runs behind the `finalizing` channel, never on the response path.** On `vai.fulfillment.complete` (scalars as D5) the listener, within `TRANSFER_FINALIZE_WAIT_S` (20 s, inside the shipped sender's single 30 s complete wait, `aim-data:app/services/fulfillment_service.py:236-248`): if a completion job for this session has already **succeeded**, answers idempotently with the existing grant (the shipped `:329-340` behaviour); if one has **failed** on a member, answers the refusal naming the unverified members (the device re-streams them); otherwise starts the job if none is running and answers **`finalizing`**. The device re-sends complete every `TRANSFER_RETRY_AFTER_S` within `TRANSFER_COMPLETE_RETRY_BUDGET_S` (§2). The **completion job** (finalizer executor, §3.1) waits for the session's finalizer to drain, requires every `transfer_session_members` row to carry `verified_at`, then — **outside any DB transaction, with the per-order lock not held** — performs one `HEAD` per member (batched, concurrency 16 on the sweep executor; AC-D4 bounds the 21,000-member sweep) comparing ETag and size to the recorded values; any miss resets that member (under the lock) and the job fails naming it. Only then, in one short transaction under the per-order lock, it re-reads the member rows (every `verified_at` and recorded ETag must be unchanged since the sweep and the session still `receiving`), writes `order_delivery_members` (with the final key as `staged path`), marks the order `delivered` and issues the grant; a chunk arriving while the job runs is refused (`finalizing`) so the member set cannot change under it. The shipped ACK-timeout monitor (armed at `app/api/v1/endpoints/trust_websocket.py:1400`, cancelled on complete/error at `trust_websocket.py:1257-1258` and on chunk arrival at `fulfillment_listener_service.py:220`; the monitor body `fulfillment_listener_service.py:879-922`) is cancelled by the complete message and stays cancelled while the job runs. **Deliberate deviation from D5, stated for the vote:** D5 re-hashes every member a second time at completion; against the store that would read the whole set twice (up to 64 GiB) from R2 for no additional guarantee, because each member was already hashed *from the store* at finalization, its key names the expected hash, the store's objects are immutable except by an overwrite under our own bucket-scoped credential, and the ETag recorded at finalization changes on any overwrite. The `HEAD` check detects deletion, replacement and truncation; it does not detect an adversary with our credential, which no re-hash would either. **Caveat, stated:** an object's ETag is store metadata — for a multipart object it is derived from the parts' MD5s (R2 behaviour UNVERIFIED until AC-D1) — so equivalence to a SHA-256 re-hash holds only up to MD5 collision resistance against a writer that already holds our credential; that is inside the excluded trust boundary. Decided by Max as D-A1 (§6); the fallback, recorded for the record, is a second streamed re-hash of every member (same code path as §3.4) at ≈ 2× store read time and no design change.

### 3.6 Buyer path (interim, shipped by chunk D)
As D5 — one order-scoped grant per order and purchased version; entitlement per set; the order endpoint returns the member list — with the fetch restated: each member address is `GET /api/v1/orders/{order_id}/members/{member_index}` under the grant (header `X-Download-Token`, the shipped convention at `fulfillment_download.py:43-58`, plus the authenticated buyer); the endpoint validates grant, **grant remaining validity ≥ `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` + 60 s** (else refused `grant_expiring`: the buyer renews the grant, which counts as one download as D5 says, so an issued URL can never outlive its grant), order `delivered`, staging state `active` (§3.7), member in `order_delivery_members`, then — **reconcile before use, the delivery leg of §3.3** — performs a `HEAD` on the member's final key (chunk-path budget, 5/15 s, no retry) and compares ETag and size to the values recorded in `order_delivery_members`: a miss (deleted, replaced, truncated out of band after `delivered`) marks that member `unavailable` on `order_delivery_members`, answers `member_unavailable` (a named terminal state: the order page shows "file unavailable — support notified" and a support ticket is opened with the order id and index; no member repair exists after `delivered`, D5 immutability), and consumes no budget; on a match it **re-checks the grant's remaining validity** (still ≥ `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` + 60 s — the HEAD is bounded by 20 s and clock skew by the 60 s margin, so an issued URL can never outlive its grant) and the buyer's byte budget, and answers **`302` to a presigned R2 GET** with TTL `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` (300 s), `response-content-disposition: attachment; filename*=UTF-8''<basename(relative_path), RFC 5987 percent-encoded>` plus an ASCII `filename=` fallback (RFC 6266, the workspace amendment W-D4 rule), `response-content-type: application/octet-stream`; the redirect response itself carries `Cache-Control: no-store` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`. ai.market never proxies member bytes (R2 serves them, no egress fee); a presigned URL is single-member, expires in minutes, and discloses nothing but that member: the key carries the order id, generation, member index and hash, and the disposition parameter carries the basename of the buyer's own paid file — no seller or buyer identity and no directory path. **Budget — restating D5's "bounded by a per-client byte budget" honestly, as this amendment's change to D5:** `DOWNLOAD_GRANT_BYTES_PER_HOUR` (deferred by Gate 2 §6; renamed in meaning, not in name) is an **issuance** budget: the sum of the sizes of members for which a presigned URL was issued to one **buyer, across all of that buyer's grants and orders**, in the trailing hour, may not exceed it. **Window mechanism:** a **tumbling hourly bucket** — `buyer_download_meter(buyer_id, window_start, bytes_issued)`, `window_start` = the current hour floored, one row per buyer per hour; "trailing hour" therefore means the current clock hour, and a buyer can issue up to 2× the value across an hour edge (stated; acceptable for a soft request-volume guard over the buyer's own paid members). **Ordering:** the presigned URL is generated first (a local HMAC, no network), then in one short transaction the bucket row — `UNIQUE (buyer_id, window_start)`, created by an upsert (`INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`) and then locked `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, so a concurrent first request for the same hour serializes on the row rather than on a missing one — `bytes_issued + size ≤ DOWNLOAD_GRANT_BYTES_PER_HOUR` is checked and the size added, and only then is the redirect returned — so concurrent requests cannot overshoot, a failed presign consumes no budget, and a response lost after the commit is counted (conservative). Value **192 GiB** = 3 × `TRANSFER_MAX_TOTAL_BYTES`. What it does *not* bound, stated plainly: a presigned URL is replayable until it expires and R2 keeps no per-URL counter that ai.market reads, so **bytes actually served** are additionally bounded only by `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` × the client's bandwidth per issued URL (at 1 Gbit/s, ≈ 37 GB per URL per 300 s — and only of a member the buyer has paid for). R2 charges no egress, so the residual exposure is R2 request volume, not money. If a served-byte bound is required, the buyer path must be a backend stream that reads from R2 and meters bytes per buyer (GLM R1 BETTER) — decided by Max as **D-A3** for the presign model (§6, Event Ledger `a3a64f39`); the backend-stream alternative is recorded for the record. The legacy single-file download at `fulfillment_download.py` is byte-identical. The order page (`ai-market-frontend app/dashboard/orders/[id]/page.tsx`, Gate 2 §2) renders the member list with per-file size, hash and a download control that follows the redirect.

### 3.7 Sweeps and retention
**Durable staging state (new, the D5 per-order lock made durable across deferred deletes):** a row `order_staging(order_id, generation UUID, state ∈ {active, purging, purged}, state_changed_at)` is created with the first manifest-mode session for an order; every key of the order carries its `generation`. Session creation (§3.2), chunk handling, completion and grant issuance/renewal (§3.6) all read it under the per-order lock and proceed only on `active`; `purging` refuses (session: `listener_busy` with retry-after; grant: `503` retry-after — the shipped grant path is unchanged for legacy orders); `purged` on an abandoned order lets the next metadata mint a fresh generation and start empty; `purged` on a delivered order is terminal for downloads (retention over — D5's `max(delivered_at + 24 h, last grant expiry)` plus one presign TTL has elapsed, so no grant and no issued URL can still be valid). One scheduler job (Celery beat, `app/tasks/multi_file_versions.py` beside chunk C's sweeps, Gate 2 R10) runs the shipped single-file routines unchanged and, over the `orders/` prefix only, the three D5 rules, each as **CAS first, delete second**: under the per-order lock the sweep re-checks its predicate and CAS-sets `active → purging` (capturing `generation`); after the lock is released it deletes the captured generation's keys and aborts its open uploads; then CAS-sets `purging → purged`. A delete that fails leaves `purging`, and the next run completes it; a delete can never touch a newer generation because new keys carry a new UUID. The rules: (i) `delivered` orders — after `max(delivered_at + 24 h, expiry of the last valid grant) + TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S`, with no grant currently valid (a grant cannot be issued or renewed once the CAS has run, and §3.6 refuses a presign in a grant's final `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` + 60 s, so no live URL can point into a purged generation); (ii) sessions past `TRANSFER_SESSION_TTL_S` (idle) — under the lock mark the session `TIMED_OUT` (the shipped mark, `:811`/`:832`, now with a caller) and **reset every unverified member of that session** (chunk and part receipts deleted), then abort those members' open uploads by their persisted `upload_id`s; verified objects are retained for resume and the staging state stays `active`; (iii) abandoned orders past `ABANDONED_ORDER_TTL_S` (no `receiving` session, no `delivered_at`, no `order_delivery_members`) — CAS `active → purging`, abort every open upload under the generation, `delete_prefix`, CAS `→ purged`. Members whose open upload exceeds `TRANSFER_UPLOAD_MAX_AGE_S` (§3.4) are reset and aborted by the same job. **Backstop:** an R2 bucket lifecycle rule "abort incomplete multipart uploads after 8 days" on the `orders/` prefix, set by Max in the dashboard as a §8 checkpoint. It is unreachable for any upload the listener still considers live (the 3-day lease is enforced by the listener on every message, not only by the scheduler), it cannot delete a completed object, and if it ever does abort an upload the listener learns it as `NoSuchUpload` and resets the member (§3.1) — so even a dead scheduler leaves nothing that the reconcile-before-use invariant (§3.3) does not reset before the receipts are used. Immutability from `delivered` holds: after that commit nothing writes under the order's generation prefix except sweep (i).

## 4. Acceptance criteria (delta to Gate 1 AC4; the rest of AC4 stands)
- **AC-D1 (store facts):** before any other chunk D test, a live check against `aimarket-listing-assets` records: a 5 MiB part accepted, a 4 MiB non-final part refused by the store, a 2 GiB object completed from 256 parts, `HEAD` ETag stable across reads, presigned GET honouring `response-content-disposition`; evidence without credentials.
- **AC-D2 (window and ACK):** the **chunk D sender** (the pinned 4-chunk window, plan order and 30 s waits unchanged; response correlation and the four refusal handlers added) transfers a one-part member, a three-part member, a five-chunk member followed by a 130-chunk member (part boundaries not window-aligned) and a sequence of twenty small members without stalling — non-boundary ACKs arrive on acceptance, the part-closing response after the part commits; **injected `create_multipart` + `UploadPart` latency totalling 12 s** on a member's first part still completes, injected latency of 35 s fails the session with a session-level error (no receipt written, no corruption) and the order resumes in a new session with verified members retained; **injected finalization latency of 120 s** (a slow `CompleteMultipartUpload` or re-hash read) delays no ACK and no chunk response, and a `complete` sent during it is answered `finalizing` within `TRANSFER_FINALIZE_WAIT_S` then succeeds on the sender's retry; a refusal (`member_reset`) returned at a non-ACK boundary is consumed by the chunk D sender at once (metadata re-sent within one round trip); **the pinned, unmodified sender** meeting `finalizing` or `member_reset` fails the session and the order resumes with verified members retained (safe fallback); **a window of four one-chunk members** (four `put_object` flushes) completes inside the deadline and an injected 6 s per flush fails it as a session-level error; **executor saturation:** at the 32-session admission limit with 120 s finalizer reads injected in every session, a plan-order sender streaming part-closing chunks in one session never exceeds the 30 s ACK timeout, and the 33rd session is refused `listener_busy` at metadata, never after chunks were accepted; chunks arrive in any order within a part and the part uploads once; a chunk for a later part is refused `out_of_window` and the session stays open; a resend of a chunk in a receipted part is acknowledged with no store call (asserted by a call counter); a conflicting resend is refused.
- **AC-D3 (durability):** fault injection before/after `UploadPart` returns, before/after the receipt transaction, between `CompleteMultipartUpload` and `verified_at`, and before/after the delivered/grant commit shows either no receipt and a member re-streamed, or receipts whose parts exist under the open upload (or object) and hash to the manifest; **aborting an unverified member's open upload out-of-band while the session is `receiving`** (harness `abort_multipart`, simulating the lifecycle rule with the scheduler stopped) is detected on the next store use for that member (`NoSuchUpload` on `UploadPart`/`CompleteMultipartUpload`, or the `upload_id` check at resume), the member is reset, the device's next chunk is answered `member_reset`, the device re-sends metadata and re-streams the member, no chunk is acknowledged from the stale receipts after the member's reset, no member is verified or delivered from receipts alone, and the order still completes with no session-level error; **an abort immediately after the receipt transaction with no further message from the device** (and the scheduler stopped) leaves stale receipts that are reset at the next resume before `verified_member_indices` is computed, and are never used for finalization or completion; a store **timeout** on the chunk path yields a session-level error, no receipt, and a resumable order; a resume finding a duplicate open upload for one member key adopts the persisted `upload_id` and aborts the other; **a verified member's object deleted between sessions** is found by the resume HEAD, the member reset and re-streamed, and `verified_member_indices` never lists it; an upload older than `TRANSFER_UPLOAD_MAX_AGE_S` is aborted and its member reset; no member is ever `verified_at` with a missing or mismatching object; **a backend replica restart that loses the part buffer** between two committed parts of one member is followed by the device's reconnect + metadata replay (a continuation of the still-`receiving` session, §3.2) that continues that member under the persisted `upload_id` (parts already receipted are acknowledged without a store write, the next part uploads, `CompleteMultipartUpload` succeeds with all ETags) — never a `NoSuchPart` loop; the 10,000-member / 500,000-chunk simulation completes with O(1) per-chunk work and ≤ 129 rows per part transaction.
- **AC-D4 (store-side verification):** an object overwritten in the store after `verified_at` (test harness, our credential) is caught at completion by the `HEAD` check, the member reset and re-streamed; a member whose completed object hashes differently from the manifest is deleted and reset; **completion latency:** on the 21,000-member fixture with the HEAD sweep paced to 120 s, every `complete` message is answered within `TRANSFER_FINALIZE_WAIT_S` (`finalizing`, then the grant), the chunk D sender's retries reach the grant without ever sending `vai.fulfillment.error`, the pinned sender's single 30 s wait is never exceeded by a response, the job completes in bounded time (assert < 120 s against the stub), and the completion transaction holds the per-order lock for a bounded number of row operations, no store calls; a chunk sent while the job runs is refused `finalizing`.
- **AC-D5 (buyer):** every member downloads via the redirect under one grant; the presigned URL expires at `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S`; replaying the same `Location` within the TTL is served by the store and counted once (the documented issuance semantics); a URL for another order's member is a 404 before any presign; **a delivered member's object deleted or replaced out of band** is caught by the issuance HEAD — no `302`, no `bytes_issued` increment, the member marked `unavailable`, `member_unavailable` returned and a support ticket opened; a matching HEAD is one Class B call per download; the budget is enforced **per buyer across two grants on two orders** and under concurrent issuance (N parallel requests never exceed the value in aggregate), refusing above `DOWNLOAD_GRANT_BYTES_PER_HOUR` naming the value; a bucket edge resets the meter (≤ 2× across the edge, as stated) and a presign that fails before reservation consumes no budget; a presign requested inside a grant's final `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` + 60 s is refused `grant_expiring` and succeeds after renewal; the redirect carries `Cache-Control: no-store`; filenames containing non-ASCII, `%`, `;` and `"` yield one correctly percent-encoded `filename*` and a safe ASCII fallback; a grant is refused while the order's staging state is not `active`; the legacy single-file download is byte-identical; no response ever carries the bucket name, credential or a key outside the redirect `Location`.

## 5. Risks
- **Part buffer in memory:** a replica restart loses at most one part per open session; bounded by `TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS`; recovery is member-level re-streaming, which is slower than D5's chunk-level repair for members > 8 MiB — accepted because it needs no wire change.
- **Twice the bytes through R2:** every member is written once and read once at finalization (Class A writes ≈ $4.50/million, Class B reads ≈ $0.36/million; no egress fee). At the trigger scale (21,000 members, mostly single-part) ≈ 21,000 writes + 21,000 reads + 21,000 HEADs ≈ $0.10 per delivery, plus ≈ $0.008 per resume.
- **Presigned URLs are bearer tokens for five minutes** for one paid member, replayable within the TTL; mitigated by TTL, the per-buyer issuance budget, `no-store` and the buyer-authenticated redirect; the served-byte exposure is stated in §3.6 and is Max's D-A3.
- **Lifecycle backstop is dashboard-configured** (not code): recorded as a §8 checkpoint and in `listing-asset-store.md`; the listener's own 3-day lease makes correctness independent of it.
- **Deployment:** the Celery service that runs the sweeps needs the four `LISTING_ASSET_*` variables (today mirrored only to `ai-market-backend`, `listing-asset-store.md`); without them the sweeps no-op and staging leaks (cost, not data loss) — §8 checkpoint.
