## 1. Why D5 must be restated

Gate 1 D5 stages every member of a directory order under `STAGING_DIR/orders/<order_id>/` with fsync/rename semantics and serves member fetches from that directory. Gate 2 §2/§4.4 record that `STAGING_DIR` is `/tmp/fulfillment` on a Railway container with **no volume**: every in-flight and delivered order would be lost on the next deploy. Max chose an ai.market-owned Cloudflare R2 bucket over a Railway volume (D-G2-1). Object storage has no directories, no partial files, no rename and no fsync; multipart uploads have minimum part sizes; the buyer can be handed a presigned URL instead of a stream. So the *mechanics* of D5 — receive, verify, finalize, sweep, serve — must be restated against the store. The *product rules* of D5 do not change: the whole set is delivered under the purchased version's manifest; nothing is `delivered` until every member matches; the device never selects the mode; resume is server-authoritative; one open session per order; immutability from `delivered`; entitlement per set.

## 3. D5 restated against the store (normative where it differs from Gate 1)

### 3.1 Store client and placement
Chunk D extends chunk C0's `app/services/listing_asset_store.py` (the one bounded client for `LISTING_ASSET_BUCKET`, Gate 2 §4.2a) with `create_multipart`, `upload_part`, `complete_multipart`, `abort_multipart`, `list_multipart_uploads(prefix)`, `head`, `get_stream`, `delete_prefix` and `presign_get(key, ttl, response_content_disposition)`; no other module talks to the bucket. boto3 is synchronous: every store call runs in the listener's thread pool (`asyncio.to_thread`). On the **chunk path** (`UploadPart`/`PutObject` inside `handle_chunk`) the budget is `TRANSFER_STORE_TIMEOUT_S` = 5 s connect / 20 s read with **no retry** — the total must stay inside the shipped sender's 30 s ACK timeout (`aim-data:app/services/fulfillment_service.py:58`, `:457-488`), because the ACK that closes a part is returned only after the part commits (§3.3); elsewhere (HEAD sweeps, finalization read, sweeps) one retry is allowed. **Store error classes:** `NoSuchUpload`/`NoSuchPart`/`InvalidPart` (on `UploadPart`/`CompleteMultipartUpload`/`ListParts`) and `NoSuchKey`/`404` on a key the listener expects (final key of a `verified_at` member) are **member resets** (§3.4 repair: chunk and part receipts deleted, counters and `upload_id` cleared, member reported unverified) — they mean the store no longer holds what the receipts claim; every other store failure (timeout, 5xx, auth) is a **session-level** error (the order stays resumable; `vai.fulfillment.error` semantics of D5 unchanged). Credentials are the four Infisical-managed variables of `listing-asset-store.md`; the token is bucket-scoped Object Read & Write, so a compromised backend can reach only this bucket. Key prefixes owned by chunk D: `orders/` only; chunk D's sweeps never read or delete outside it (the same prefix-ownership rule as the workspace amendment W-D3 (iii)).

### 3.2 Session start (metadata)
As D5, with two additions. (a) **Admission bound:** the listener holds at most `TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS` open manifest-mode sessions per backend replica (one in-memory part buffer each); a metadata message beyond that is refused before session creation with `listener_busy`, the order stays undelivered and the device retries after `TRANSFER_RETRY_AFTER_S` — a refusal payload value, not a new message. (b) **Resume reconciliation from the store, under the per-order lock** (everywhere in this document "the per-order lock" is the shipped `orders`-row `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` of `_lock_owned_order`, `fulfillment_listener_service.py:567`, taken at `:213`/`:320`/`:498`/`:651` — a DB row lock, so the out-of-process sweeps of §3.7 take exactly the same lock)**:** the order's staging state must be `active` (§3.7; `purging` → refused with `listener_busy`, `purged` → a fresh generation is minted, every member reset, and the session starts empty); for every member with `verified_at`, `HEAD` on its final key must return the recorded ETag and size, else the member is reset (D5 (c), now O(verified members) HEAD calls at session start — 21,000 Class B operations at the trigger scale, ≈ $0.008); for every unverified member with part receipts, its persisted `upload_id` must appear in `list_multipart_uploads(prefix)` (else the member is reset), and every open upload under the generation prefix that is not a persisted `upload_id` is aborted (**invariant: at most one open multipart upload per member key**, adopted by persisted id, never by key alone). **The orphan test is evaluated against durable state only** — the DB rows, never the replica's in-memory registry — so a replica restart that loses the part buffer never aborts an upload whose parts are receipted: the buffer is gone, the `upload_id` and part receipts are not, and the next part uploads under the same id. `verified_member_indices` is computed only from members that passed the HEAD.

### 3.3 Chunk arrival: part window
The frozen chunk plan (D5) additionally groups each member's chunks into **parts** of `TRANSFER_PART_BYTES` (8 MiB = 128 chunks of 65,536 bytes; the last part of a member is shorter; a member of ≤ `TRANSFER_PART_BYTES` is one part uploaded by `PutObject`). Per open session the listener keeps **one in-memory part buffer** with a per-chunk bitmap. Order of operations per chunk: validate `chunk_index`/`member_index`/`byte_offset`/length against the plan → verify `chunk_sha256` → look up the chunk receipt: present and equal → acknowledged, nothing written; present and different → refused; absent and the chunk belongs to the **current part** (the lowest part of the lowest unverified member with no part receipt, or the part currently buffered) → copied into the buffer at its offset (a resend within the buffer overwrites identical bytes); absent and the chunk belongs to a **later** part → refused with `out_of_window` (session stays open; the device re-sends metadata and resumes, §2). **Deliberate deviation from D5, stated for the vote:** D5 tolerates arbitrary out-of-order arrival; this amendment tolerates it only within the current part, because a multipart part must be complete before it can be written. The plan-order sender never triggers `out_of_window` except after a member reset (below). **ACK cadence:** the listener acknowledges on the shipped `ACK_WINDOW` (every 4 chunks, `fulfillment_listener_service.py:276-284`; 128 chunks = 32 exactly aligned windows, so no window straddles two parts) as soon as the chunks are accepted into the buffer — an ACK is backpressure, not durability — **except the window that closes a part:** the shipped serial handler (`trust_websocket.py:776-777`, `:1282`) returns the ACK from `handle_chunk`, so that ACK is necessarily returned after `UploadPart` has returned. This is why the chunk-path store budget (§3.1) is bounded inside the sender's 30 s ACK timeout; a `UploadPart` that exceeds it answers the chunk with a session-level error, the shipped sender fails the session, and the order resumes by a new session (verified members retained by the §3.2 HEAD check; that session's unverified receipts swept and re-streamed). **Member reset during an open session:** when a member is reset (§3.1 error class, §3.4 mismatch, lease), the listener answers the device's next chunk for that member with `member_reset`; the device re-sends metadata and resumes (§2). When the buffer's bitmap is full, the listener calls `UploadPart` (creating the multipart upload on the member's first part and persisting its `upload_id`; or `PutObject` for a single-part member), and on success inserts, **in one transaction**, the part receipt with the returned ETag and the part's chunk receipts, advancing `bytes_received` and `chunks_received` on `transfer_session_members`. **A chunk receipt therefore still implies durable bytes** (in the store, under an open multipart upload or a completed object) — stated precisely as a **reconcile-before-use invariant**, not cross-store atomicity: a receipt records an observed successful store write; an out-of-band loss afterwards (a lifecycle or manual abort, a deleted object) can leave receipts pointing at nothing until the next use, and every use of receipts is preceded by a store check that resets the member on loss — resume (§3.2(b): `upload_id` in `list_multipart_uploads`, HEAD on verified keys), finalization (§3.4: `NoSuchUpload`/`NoSuchPart` on `CompleteMultipartUpload`), completion (§3.5: HEAD), and the listener's own aborts (§3.7, lease), which delete the member's receipts in the same step. Receipts are therefore never *used* for `verified_member_indices`, finalization or delivery without a store check that would expose the loss; with a dead scheduler and a silent sender the stale receipts simply wait for the next message or resume, where they are reset. A crash before `UploadPart` returns loses only the buffer: no receipts exist for those chunks, the device sees the member unverified on the next metadata response and re-streams it from its first chunk; chunks of already-receipted parts are acknowledged idempotently without any store write, so a re-stream costs wire but not store. Per-chunk work is O(1) (one bitmap set and one memcpy; one indexed receipt lookup) and per-part work is one store call and one transaction of ≤ 129 rows. Zero-byte member: one zero-length `PutObject` (matches the shipped sender's single empty chunk).

### 3.4 Member finalization
When a member's part receipts equal its expected part count, the listener calls `CompleteMultipartUpload` with the recorded ETags in order (or the `PutObject` already completed it), then **streams the object back from the store and hashes it**; equal to the manifest `sha256` → `verified_at`, the object's ETag and size are recorded on `transfer_session_members`; different → `DeleteObject`, receipts and part receipts for that member deleted, counters reset, member reported unverified (D5's repair rule; the device streams it again). The store read is the equivalent of D5's "hash from wherever the bytes are": it verifies what the store holds, not what the listener sent. `TRANSFER_MAX_MEMBER_BYTES` (2 GiB) → at most 256 parts per member, well under R2's 10,000. Reconciliation (b) of D5 becomes: any member whose part receipts are complete and whose `verified_at` is null is finalized as above on the next message for the session (bounded to that message's member plus members left in that state by a crash); a crash between `CompleteMultipartUpload` and `verified_at` is repaired by a `HEAD` (object present → re-hash and record; absent → reset). **Absolute upload lease:** a member's open upload older than `TRANSFER_UPLOAD_MAX_AGE_S` (3 days from `create_multipart`) is aborted by the listener on the next message or sweep and the member reset — so a live-but-slow transfer is re-streamed member by member rather than ever meeting the 8-day lifecycle rule (§3.7), and D5's 6-hour idle TTL keeps its meaning.

### 3.5 Completion transaction
On `vai.fulfillment.complete` (scalars as D5), the listener requires every `transfer_session_members` row to carry `verified_at`, then — **outside any DB transaction, with the per-order lock not held** — performs one `HEAD` per member (batched, concurrency 16; AC-D4 bounds the 21,000-member sweep) comparing ETag and size to the recorded values; any miss resets that member (under the lock) and refuses completion (session stays open). Only then, in one short transaction under the per-order lock, it re-reads the member rows (every `verified_at` and recorded ETag must be unchanged since the sweep and the session still `receiving`), writes `order_delivery_members` (with the final key as `staged path`), marks the order `delivered` and issues the grant. The shipped ACK-timeout monitor (`:744-751`) is not armed during the sweep because no chunk is outstanding; a completion refused by the timeout is retried by the device's ordinary complete retry (D5, idempotent). **Deliberate deviation from D5, stated for the vote:** D5 re-hashes every member a second time at completion; against the store that would read the whole set twice (up to 64 GiB) from R2 for no additional guarantee, because each member was already hashed *from the store* at finalization, its key names the expected hash, the store's objects are immutable except by an overwrite under our own bucket-scoped credential, and the ETag recorded at finalization changes on any overwrite. The `HEAD` check detects deletion, replacement and truncation; it does not detect an adversary with our credential, which no re-hash would either. **Caveat, stated:** an object's ETag is store metadata — for a multipart object it is derived from the parts' MD5s (R2 behaviour UNVERIFIED until AC-D1) — so equivalence to a SHA-256 re-hash holds only up to MD5 collision resistance against a writer that already holds our credential; that is inside the excluded trust boundary. Decided by Max as D-A1 (§6); the fallback, recorded for the record, is a second streamed re-hash of every member (same code path as §3.4) at ≈ 2× store read time and no design change.

### 3.6 Buyer path (interim, shipped by chunk D)
As D5 — one order-scoped grant per order and purchased version; entitlement per set; the order endpoint returns the member list — with the fetch restated: each member address is `GET /api/v1/orders/{order_id}/members/{member_index}` under the grant (header `X-Download-Token`, the shipped convention at `fulfillment_download.py:43-58`, plus the authenticated buyer); the endpoint validates grant, **grant remaining validity ≥ `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` + 60 s** (else refused `grant_expiring`: the buyer renews the grant, which counts as one download as D5 says, so an issued URL can never outlive its grant), order `delivered`, staging state `active` (§3.7), member in `order_delivery_members`, and the buyer's byte budget, then answers **`302` to a presigned R2 GET** with TTL `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` (300 s), `response-content-disposition: attachment; filename*=UTF-8''<basename(relative_path), RFC 5987 percent-encoded>` plus an ASCII `filename=` fallback (RFC 6266, the workspace amendment W-D4 rule), `response-content-type: application/octet-stream`; the redirect response itself carries `Cache-Control: no-store` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`. ai.market never proxies member bytes (R2 serves them, no egress fee); a presigned URL is single-member, expires in minutes, and discloses nothing but that member: the key carries the order id, generation, member index and hash, and the disposition parameter carries the basename of the buyer's own paid file — no seller or buyer identity and no directory path. **Budget — restating D5's "bounded by a per-client byte budget" honestly, as this amendment's change to D5:** `DOWNLOAD_GRANT_BYTES_PER_HOUR` (deferred by Gate 2 §6; renamed in meaning, not in name) is an **issuance** budget: the sum of the sizes of members for which a presigned URL was issued to one **buyer, across all of that buyer's grants and orders**, in the trailing hour, may not exceed it. **Window mechanism:** a **tumbling hourly bucket** — `buyer_download_meter(buyer_id, window_start, bytes_issued)`, `window_start` = the current hour floored, one row per buyer per hour; "trailing hour" therefore means the current clock hour, and a buyer can issue up to 2× the value across an hour edge (stated; acceptable for a soft request-volume guard over the buyer's own paid members). **Ordering:** the presigned URL is generated first (a local HMAC, no network), then in one short transaction the bucket row is locked (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, inserted if absent), `bytes_issued + size ≤ DOWNLOAD_GRANT_BYTES_PER_HOUR` is checked and the size added, and only then is the redirect returned — so concurrent requests cannot overshoot, a failed presign consumes no budget, and a response lost after the commit is counted (conservative). Value **192 GiB** = 3 × `TRANSFER_MAX_TOTAL_BYTES`. What it does *not* bound, stated plainly: a presigned URL is replayable until it expires and R2 keeps no per-URL counter that ai.market reads, so **bytes actually served** are additionally bounded only by `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` × the client's bandwidth per issued URL (at 1 Gbit/s, ≈ 37 GB per URL per 300 s — and only of a member the buyer has paid for). R2 charges no egress, so the residual exposure is R2 request volume, not money. If a served-byte bound is required, the buyer path must be a backend stream that reads from R2 and meters bytes per buyer (GLM R1 BETTER) — decided by Max as **D-A3** for the presign model (§6, Event Ledger `a3a64f39`); the backend-stream alternative is recorded for the record. The legacy single-file download at `fulfillment_download.py` is byte-identical. The order page (`ai-market-frontend app/dashboard/orders/[id]/page.tsx`, Gate 2 §2) renders the member list with per-file size, hash and a download control that follows the redirect.

### 3.7 Sweeps and retention
**Durable staging state (new, the D5 per-order lock made durable across deferred deletes):** a row `order_staging(order_id, generation UUID, state ∈ {active, purging, purged}, state_changed_at)` is created with the first manifest-mode session for an order; every key of the order carries its `generation`. Session creation (§3.2), chunk handling, completion and grant issuance/renewal (§3.6) all read it under the per-order lock and proceed only on `active`; `purging` refuses (session: `listener_busy` with retry-after; grant: `503` retry-after — the shipped grant path is unchanged for legacy orders); `purged` on an abandoned order lets the next metadata mint a fresh generation and start empty; `purged` on a delivered order is terminal for downloads (retention over — D5's `max(delivered_at + 24 h, last grant expiry)` plus one presign TTL has elapsed, so no grant and no issued URL can still be valid). One scheduler job (Celery beat, `app/tasks/multi_file_versions.py` beside chunk C's sweeps, Gate 2 R10) runs the shipped single-file routines unchanged and, over the `orders/` prefix only, the three D5 rules, each as **CAS first, delete second**: under the per-order lock the sweep re-checks its predicate and CAS-sets `active → purging` (capturing `generation`); after the lock is released it deletes the captured generation's keys and aborts its open uploads; then CAS-sets `purging → purged`. A delete that fails leaves `purging`, and the next run completes it; a delete can never touch a newer generation because new keys carry a new UUID. The rules: (i) `delivered` orders — after `max(delivered_at + 24 h, expiry of the last valid grant) + TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S`, with no grant currently valid (a grant cannot be issued or renewed once the CAS has run, and §3.6 refuses a presign in a grant's final `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` + 60 s, so no live URL can point into a purged generation); (ii) sessions past `TRANSFER_SESSION_TTL_S` (idle) — under the lock mark the session `TIMED_OUT` (the shipped mark, `:811`/`:832`, now with a caller) and **reset every unverified member of that session** (chunk and part receipts deleted), then abort those members' open uploads by their persisted `upload_id`s; verified objects are retained for resume and the staging state stays `active`; (iii) abandoned orders past `ABANDONED_ORDER_TTL_S` (no `receiving` session, no `delivered_at`, no `order_delivery_members`) — CAS `active → purging`, abort every open upload under the generation, `delete_prefix`, CAS `→ purged`. Members whose open upload exceeds `TRANSFER_UPLOAD_MAX_AGE_S` (§3.4) are reset and aborted by the same job. **Backstop:** an R2 bucket lifecycle rule "abort incomplete multipart uploads after 8 days" on the `orders/` prefix, set by Max in the dashboard as a §8 checkpoint. It is unreachable for any upload the listener still considers live (the 3-day lease is enforced by the listener on every message, not only by the scheduler), it cannot delete a completed object, and if it ever does abort an upload the listener learns it as `NoSuchUpload` and resets the member (§3.1) — so even a dead scheduler leaves nothing that the reconcile-before-use invariant (§3.3) does not reset before the receipts are used. Immutability from `delivered` holds: after that commit nothing writes under the order's generation prefix except sweep (i).

## 5. Risks
- **Part buffer in memory:** a replica restart loses at most one part per open session; bounded by `TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS`; recovery is member-level re-streaming, which is slower than D5's chunk-level repair for members > 8 MiB — accepted because it needs no wire change.
- **Twice the bytes through R2:** every member is written once and read once at finalization (Class A writes ≈ $4.50/million, Class B reads ≈ $0.36/million; no egress fee). At the trigger scale (21,000 members, mostly single-part) ≈ 21,000 writes + 21,000 reads + 21,000 HEADs ≈ $0.10 per delivery, plus ≈ $0.008 per resume.
- **Presigned URLs are bearer tokens for five minutes** for one paid member, replayable within the TTL; mitigated by TTL, the per-buyer issuance budget, `no-store` and the buyer-authenticated redirect; the served-byte exposure is stated in §3.6 and is Max's D-A3.
- **Lifecycle backstop is dashboard-configured** (not code): recorded as a §8 checkpoint and in `listing-asset-store.md`; the listener's own 3-day lease makes correctness independent of it.
- **Deployment:** the Celery service that runs the sweeps needs the four `LISTING_ASSET_*` variables (today mirrored only to `ai-market-backend`, `listing-asset-store.md`); without them the sweeps no-op and staging leaks (cost, not data loss) — §8 checkpoint.
