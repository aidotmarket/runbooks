## 1. Why D5 must be restated

Gate 1 D5 stages every member of a directory order under `STAGING_DIR/orders/<order_id>/` with fsync/rename semantics and serves member fetches from that directory. Gate 2 §2/§4.4 record that `STAGING_DIR` is `/tmp/fulfillment` on a Railway container with **no volume**: every in-flight and delivered order would be lost on the next deploy. Max chose an ai.market-owned Cloudflare R2 bucket over a Railway volume (D-G2-1). Object storage has no directories, no partial files, no rename and no fsync; multipart uploads have minimum part sizes; the buyer can be handed a presigned URL instead of a stream. So the *mechanics* of D5 — receive, verify, finalize, sweep, serve — must be restated against the store. The *product rules* of D5 do not change: the whole set is delivered under the purchased version's manifest; nothing is `delivered` until every member matches; the device never selects the mode; resume is server-authoritative; one open session per order; immutability from `delivered`; entitlement per set.

## 3. D5 restated against the store (normative where it differs from Gate 1)

### 3.1 Store client and placement
Chunk D extends chunk C0's `app/services/listing_asset_store.py` (the one bounded client for `LISTING_ASSET_BUCKET`, Gate 2 §4.2a) with `create_multipart`, `upload_part`, `complete_multipart`, `abort_multipart`, `list_multipart_uploads(prefix)`, `head`, `get_stream`, `delete_prefix` and `presign_get(key, ttl, response_content_disposition)`; no other module talks to the bucket. boto3 is synchronous: every store call runs in the listener's thread pool (`asyncio.to_thread`) with connect/read timeouts and one retry, and a store failure on the chunk path is a **session-level** error (the order stays resumable; `vai.fulfillment.error` semantics of D5 unchanged), never a member reset. Credentials are the four Infisical-managed variables of `listing-asset-store.md`; the token is bucket-scoped Object Read & Write, so a compromised backend can reach only this bucket. Key prefixes owned by chunk D: `orders/` only; chunk D's sweeps never read or delete outside it (the same prefix-ownership rule as the workspace amendment W-D3 (iii)).

### 3.2 Session start (metadata)
As D5, with two additions. (a) **Admission bound:** the listener holds at most `TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS` open manifest-mode sessions per backend replica (one in-memory part buffer each); a metadata message beyond that is refused before session creation with `listener_busy`, the order stays undelivered and the device retries after `TRANSFER_RETRY_AFTER_S` — a refusal payload value, not a new message. (b) **Resume reconciliation from the store:** for every member with `verified_at`, `HEAD` on its final key must return the recorded ETag and size, else the member is reset (D5 (c), now O(verified members) HEAD calls at session start — 21,000 Class B operations at the trigger scale, ≈ $0.008); every open multipart upload under `orders/<order_id>/` that belongs to no live session is aborted. `verified_member_indices` is computed only from members that passed the HEAD.

### 3.3 Chunk arrival: part window
The frozen chunk plan (D5) additionally groups each member's chunks into **parts** of `TRANSFER_PART_BYTES` (8 MiB = 128 chunks of 65,536 bytes; the last part of a member is shorter; a member of ≤ `TRANSFER_PART_BYTES` is one part uploaded by `PutObject`). Per open session the listener keeps **one in-memory part buffer** with a per-chunk bitmap. Order of operations per chunk: validate `chunk_index`/`member_index`/`byte_offset`/length against the plan → verify `chunk_sha256` → look up the chunk receipt: present and equal → acknowledged, nothing written; present and different → refused; absent and the chunk belongs to the **current part** (the lowest part of the lowest unverified member with no part receipt, or the part currently buffered) → copied into the buffer at its offset (a resend within the buffer overwrites identical bytes); absent and the chunk belongs to a **later** part → refused with `out_of_window` (session stays open; the plan-order sender never triggers it; a test does). When the buffer's bitmap is full, the listener calls `UploadPart` (or `PutObject` for a single-part member), and on success inserts, **in one transaction**, the part receipt with the returned ETag and the part's chunk receipts, advancing `bytes_received` and `chunks_received` on `transfer_session_members`; then it acknowledges the last chunk. **A chunk receipt therefore still implies durable bytes** (in the store, under an open multipart upload or a completed object). A crash before `UploadPart` returns loses only the buffer: no receipts exist, the device sees the member unverified on the next metadata response and re-streams it; chunks of already-receipted parts are acknowledged idempotently without any store write. Per-chunk work is O(1) (one bitmap set and one memcpy; one indexed receipt lookup) and per-part work is one store call and one transaction of ≤ 129 rows. Zero-byte member: one zero-length `PutObject` (matches the shipped sender's single empty chunk).

### 3.4 Member finalization
When a member's part receipts equal its expected part count, the listener calls `CompleteMultipartUpload` with the recorded ETags in order (or the `PutObject` already completed it), then **streams the object back from the store and hashes it**; equal to the manifest `sha256` → `verified_at`, the object's ETag and size are recorded on `transfer_session_members`; different → `DeleteObject`, receipts and part receipts for that member deleted, counters reset, member reported unverified (D5's repair rule; the device streams it again). The store read is the equivalent of D5's "hash from wherever the bytes are": it verifies what the store holds, not what the listener sent. `TRANSFER_MAX_MEMBER_BYTES` (2 GiB) → at most 256 parts per member, well under R2's 10,000. Reconciliation (b) of D5 becomes: any member whose part receipts are complete and whose `verified_at` is null is finalized as above on the next message for the session (bounded to that message's member plus members left in that state by a crash); a crash between `CompleteMultipartUpload` and `verified_at` is repaired by a `HEAD` (object present → re-hash and record; absent → reset).

### 3.5 Completion transaction
On `vai.fulfillment.complete` (scalars as D5), the listener requires every `transfer_session_members` row to carry `verified_at` and, inside the completion transaction, performs one `HEAD` per member comparing ETag and size to the recorded values; any miss resets that member and refuses completion (session stays open). Only then, in the same transaction, it writes `order_delivery_members` (with the final key as `staged path`), marks the order `delivered` and issues the grant. **Deliberate deviation from D5, stated for the vote:** D5 re-hashes every member a second time at completion; against the store that would read the whole set twice (up to 64 GiB) from R2 for no additional guarantee, because each member was already hashed *from the store* at finalization, its key names the expected hash, the store's objects are immutable except by an overwrite under our own bucket-scoped credential, and the ETag recorded at finalization changes on any overwrite. The `HEAD` check detects deletion, replacement and truncation; it does not detect an adversary with our credential, which no re-hash would either. Reviewers may reject this deviation; the fallback is a second streamed re-hash of every member (same code path as §3.4), at ≈ 2× store read time and no design change.

### 3.6 Buyer path (interim, shipped by chunk D)
As D5 — one order-scoped grant per order and purchased version; entitlement per set; the order endpoint returns the member list — with the fetch restated: each member address is `GET /api/v1/orders/{order_id}/members/{member_index}` under the grant (header `X-Download-Token`, the shipped convention at `fulfillment_download.py:43-58`, plus the authenticated buyer); the endpoint validates grant, order `delivered`, member in `order_delivery_members`, and the per-grant byte budget, then answers **`302` to a presigned R2 GET** with TTL `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` (300 s) and `response-content-disposition: attachment; filename*=UTF-8''<basename(relative_path)>`, `response-content-type: application/octet-stream`. ai.market never proxies member bytes (R2 serves them, no egress fee); a presigned URL is single-member, expires in minutes, and leaks nothing but that member (the key contains the order id, member index and hash — no seller, buyer or path information). **`DOWNLOAD_GRANT_BYTES_PER_HOUR`** (deferred by Gate 2 §6) is therefore metered at **presign issuance**: the sum of the sizes of members presigned under one grant in the trailing hour may not exceed it; value **192 GiB** = 3 × `TRANSFER_MAX_TOTAL_BYTES`, so a buyer can fetch the largest allowed set three times an hour and a runaway client is bounded; the legacy single-file download at `fulfillment_download.py` is byte-identical. The order page (`ai-market-frontend app/dashboard/orders/[id]/page.tsx`, Gate 2 §2) renders the member list with per-file size, hash and a download control that follows the redirect.

### 3.7 Sweeps and retention
One scheduler job (Celery beat, `app/tasks/multi_file_versions.py` beside chunk C's sweeps, Gate 2 R10) runs the shipped single-file routines unchanged and, over the `orders/` prefix only, the three D5 rules: (i) `delivered` orders — `delete_prefix("orders/<order_id>/")` after `max(delivered_at + 24 h, expiry of the last valid grant)`; (ii) sessions past `TRANSFER_SESSION_TTL_S` — abort every open multipart upload of that session's unverified members (verified objects retained for resume); (iii) abandoned orders past `ABANDONED_ORDER_TTL_S` (no live session, no `delivered_at`, no `order_delivery_members`) — abort open uploads, then `delete_prefix`. Every sweep takes the per-order lock (D5); object deletes run after the lock is released and are scoped to keys captured under it (the workspace amendment's reaper order), so a failed delete leaves a row that the next run completes and a resumed session never loses its own object. **Backstop:** an R2 bucket lifecycle rule "abort incomplete multipart uploads after 8 days" (> `ABANDONED_ORDER_TTL_S` = 7 days) on the `orders/` prefix, set by Max in the dashboard as a §8 checkpoint — it cannot delete a verified object (those are completed) and only bounds what a dead scheduler would otherwise leak. Immutability from `delivered` holds: after that commit nothing writes under `orders/<order_id>/` except sweep (i).

## 4. Acceptance criteria (delta to Gate 1 AC4; the rest of AC4 stands)
- **AC-D1 (store facts):** before any other chunk D test, a live check against `aimarket-listing-assets` records: a 5 MiB part accepted, a 4 MiB non-final part refused by the store, a 2 GiB object completed from 256 parts, `HEAD` ETag stable across reads, presigned GET honouring `response-content-disposition`; evidence without credentials.
- **AC-D2 (window):** chunks arrive in any order within a part and the part uploads once; a chunk for a later part is refused `out_of_window` and the session stays open; a resend of a chunk in a receipted part is acknowledged with no store call (asserted by a call counter); a conflicting resend is refused.
- **AC-D3 (durability):** fault injection before/after `UploadPart` returns, before/after the receipt transaction, between `CompleteMultipartUpload` and `verified_at`, and before/after the delivered/grant commit shows either no receipt and a member re-streamed, or receipts whose parts exist under the open upload (or object) and hash to the manifest; no member is ever `verified_at` with a missing or mismatching object; the 10,000-member / 500,000-chunk simulation completes with O(1) per-chunk work and ≤ 129 rows per part transaction.
- **AC-D4 (store-side verification):** an object overwritten in the store after `verified_at` (test harness, our credential) is caught at completion by the `HEAD` check, the member reset and re-streamed; a member whose completed object hashes differently from the manifest is deleted and reset.
- **AC-D5 (buyer):** every member downloads via the redirect under one grant; the presigned URL expires at `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S`; a URL for another order's member is a 404 before any presign; the byte budget refuses the presign above `DOWNLOAD_GRANT_BYTES_PER_HOUR` naming the value; the legacy single-file download is byte-identical; no response ever carries the bucket name, credential or a key outside the redirect `Location`.
- **AC-D6 (sweeps):** the three sweeps against a fixture of delivered, expired and abandoned orders remove exactly the rules' keys and uploads, never a sibling order's, never anything outside `orders/`; a sweep tick between expiry and resume changes no outcome; the legacy purge still runs.
- **AC-D7 (admission):** the 33rd open session on one replica is refused `listener_busy` before session creation and succeeds after one closes.

## 5. Risks
- **Part buffer in memory:** a replica restart loses at most one part per open session; bounded by `TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS`; recovery is member-level re-streaming, which is slower than D5's chunk-level repair for members > 8 MiB — accepted because it needs no wire change.
- **Twice the bytes through R2:** every member is written once and read once at finalization (Class A + B operations; no egress fee). At the trigger scale (21,000 members, mostly small) ≈ 45,000 operations ≈ $0.20 per delivery.
- **Presigned URLs are bearer tokens for five minutes** for one member; mitigated by TTL, per-grant budget and the buyer-authenticated redirect.
- **Lifecycle backstop is dashboard-configured** (not code): recorded as a §8 checkpoint and in `listing-asset-store.md`.
