## 1. Why D5 must be restated

Gate 1 D5 stages every member of a directory order under `STAGING_DIR/orders/<order_id>/` with fsync/rename semantics and serves member fetches from that directory. Gate 2 §2/§4.4 record that `STAGING_DIR` is `/tmp/fulfillment` on a Railway container with **no volume**: every in-flight and delivered order would be lost on the next deploy. Max chose an ai.market-owned Cloudflare R2 bucket over a Railway volume (D-G2-1). Object storage has no directories, no partial files, no rename and no fsync; multipart uploads have minimum part sizes; the buyer can be handed a presigned URL instead of a stream. So the *mechanics* of D5 — receive, verify, finalize, sweep, serve — must be restated against the store. The *product rules* of D5 do not change: the whole set is delivered under the purchased version's manifest; nothing is `delivered` until every member matches; the device never selects the mode; resume is server-authoritative; one open session per order; immutability from `delivered`; entitlement per set.

## 3. D5 restated against the store (normative where it differs from Gate 1)

### 3.1 Store client and placement
Chunk D extends chunk C0's `app/services/listing_asset_store.py` (the one bounded client for `LISTING_ASSET_BUCKET`, Gate 2 §4.2a) with `create_multipart`, `upload_part`, `complete_multipart`, `abort_multipart`, `list_multipart_uploads(prefix)`, `head`, `get_stream`, `delete_prefix` and `presign_get(key, ttl, response_content_disposition)`; no other module talks to the bucket. boto3 is synchronous: every store call runs on one of the named executors below. **Three bounded executors, never shared** (every store call is submitted with `loop.run_in_executor(<named executor>, …)` — never `asyncio.to_thread`, which can only reach the default pool; finalizer, completion and reconciliation *coordinators* are asyncio tasks that occupy a worker only for the duration of one store call and never while awaiting another task, so eight concurrent completion jobs cannot starve the finalizers they wait on): (1) the **chunk-path executor**, sized 2 × `TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS` and used only by `handle_chunk`'s store calls — a thread pool cannot reserve a worker per caller, so the bound is admission (§3.2(a): at most `TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS` sessions submit to it) plus headroom for one abandoned call per session (below); (2) the **finalizer executor**, `TRANSFER_FINALIZER_WORKERS` = 8 per replica, on which the per-session finalizers (§3.4) and completion jobs (§3.5) queue; (3) the **sweep executor** (`TRANSFER_SWEEP_WORKERS` = 16) for the completion HEAD sweeps, the session-start reconciliation and the scheduler's sweeps. On the **chunk path** every synchronous store call made for the chunks of one **ACK window** — the ≤ 4 chunks the sender is waiting on, which may flush up to four one-chunk members (`put_object` each) or one `create_multipart` + `upload_part` — runs under **one wall-clock deadline per window**, `TRANSFER_CHUNK_PATH_DEADLINE_S` = 20 s, **measured from the first un-acked chunk's arrival and including executor queue time** (per-call 5 s connect / 10 s read, **no retry**); **enforcement:** the deadline is what answers — when it expires the listener abandons the pending store future (the worker finishes or times out on its own within the per-call budget, which is why the pool has headroom), writes no receipt, and answers the chunk with a session-level error, so the response is at the deadline whatever the store does; it must stay inside the shipped sender's 30 s ACK timeout (`aim-data:app/services/fulfillment_service.py:57`, `:457-488`), because the response to the chunk that fills a part is returned only after the part commits (§3.3). The deadline covers the **whole part-closing response** — the store calls, the receipt transaction that follows them (129 inserted receipt rows plus one `transfer_session_members` update), *and the wait for the orders-row lock*, which every interactive path (chunk, metadata, complete, member download) acquires with `SET LOCAL lock_timeout = 5 s` and, on timeout, answers with its retryable refusal (`listener_busy` for chunk and metadata, `finalizing` for complete, `503` retry-after for the download) rather than blocking; separately, a chunk that finds `transfer_sessions.completion_state = running` (§3.5) is refused `finalizing`. **Every locked write is bounded:** no transaction holding the orders-row lock writes more than `TRANSFER_LOCKED_BATCH_ROWS` = 1,000 rows; larger resets (sweep (ii), the reconciliation job) and the completion job's `order_delivery_members` rows are written in batches with the lock released between them. Exceeding it is a session-level error. **Nothing else runs on that path:** `CompleteMultipartUpload`, the finalization re-hash read, reconciliation (b) and the completion sweep run on the other executors, never inside `handle_chunk`. Elsewhere the budget is `TRANSFER_STORE_TIMEOUT_S` (10 s / 60 s, one retry), except the buyer's issuance HEAD (§3.6), which uses the chunk-path per-call budget (5 s connect / 10 s read, no retry) so its bound (15 s) is known. These are chunk D's own client settings; the connect/read values in `listing-asset-store.md`'s verification script are that script's, not the client's. **Store error classes:** `NoSuchUpload`/`NoSuchPart`/`InvalidPart` (on `UploadPart`/`CompleteMultipartUpload`/`ListParts`) and `NoSuchKey`/`404` on a key the listener expects (final key of a `verified_at` member) are **member resets** (§3.4 repair: chunk and part receipts deleted, counters and `upload_id` cleared, member reported unverified) — they mean the store no longer holds what the receipts claim; every other store failure (timeout, 5xx, auth) is a **session-level** error (the order stays resumable; `vai.fulfillment.error` semantics of D5 unchanged). Credentials are the four Infisical-managed variables of `listing-asset-store.md`; the token is bucket-scoped Object Read & Write, so a compromised backend can reach only this bucket. Key prefixes owned by chunk D: `orders/` only; chunk D's sweeps never read or delete outside it (the same prefix-ownership rule as the workspace amendment W-D3 (iii)).

### 3.2 Session start (metadata)
As D5, with two additions. (a) **Admission bound:** the listener holds at most `TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS` open manifest-mode sessions per backend replica (one in-memory part buffer each); a metadata message beyond that is refused before session creation with `listener_busy`, the order stays undelivered and the device retries after `TRANSFER_RETRY_AFTER_S` — a refusal payload value, not a new message. (b) **Reconciliation from the store, as a background job.** (Everywhere in this document "the per-order lock" is the shipped `orders`-row `SELECT … FOR UPDATE OF o` of `_lock_owned_order` — defined at `fulfillment_listener_service.py:567`, the lock at `:579`, called at `:312` (complete) and `:493` (error), and taken inline in `handle_metadata` at `:138`; the `TransferSession` `.with_for_update()` at `:213`/`:320`/`:498`/`:651` is the *session-row* lock, a different lock that stays as shipped. **In manifest mode `handle_chunk` and the grant path newly acquire the orders-row lock** — `handle_chunk` takes it **before** the session-row lock, the shipped order "order precedes transfer on every path that acquires both" (`:491-492`); the buyer-side member endpoint takes the same `orders` row by order id (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on `orders WHERE id = :order_id`), not the seller-filtered helper — scoped to the staging-state read plus the receipt (or meter) transaction, never held across a store call, always with `lock_timeout` = 5 s (§3.1); a DB row lock, so the out-of-process sweeps of §3.7 take exactly the same lock)**. Continuation vs resume:** a metadata message for an order whose session is still `receiving` (the device reconnected after a websocket drop or a backend replica restart) is D5's idempotent replay and **continues** that session — its `transfer_id`, receipts and `upload_id`s are intact, the HEAD/`upload_id` checks below run, and streaming carries on; a **resume** is a new session created only when the previous one is terminal (`TIMED_OUT`, `FAILED`, or none): its `transfer_session_members` rows are materialized by **copying `verified_at`, `object_etag` and `object_size` from the previous session's rows** for the same `(order_id, member_index)` — no receipts, no `upload_id` — which is the store analogue of D5's "keeps `verified_at` only where they still match" once the HEAD check below re-validates them; D5's session-row sweep then deletes the old session's unverified receipts (chunk and part) and aborts their open uploads (they carry no live `upload_id` any more), while verified objects are retained. **The reconciliation job:** a continuation or resume metadata for an order that has any member to check starts one job on the sweep executor (or finds the running one) and is answered **at once** with `listener_busy` (retry-after `TRANSFER_RETRY_AFTER_S`); every metadata and chunk for that order is answered `listener_busy` until the job finishes; a **first session** has no store check but must materialize `transfer_session_members` from the purchased version's stored members (D5): a version with ≤ `TRANSFER_LOCKED_BATCH_ROWS` members is materialized inline and answered directly; a larger one is materialized by the same job in unlocked batches of `TRANSFER_LOCKED_BATCH_ROWS` (idempotent on `(session_id, member_index)`) and answered `listener_busy` until the rows exist. The job runs the store checks **unlocked** (HEADs at `TRANSFER_SWEEP_WORKERS` concurrency, one paginated `list_multipart_uploads`), then takes the per-order lock only for the reset writes and to compute `verified_member_indices`, which is cached for that continuation and returned by every later metadata replay without re-running the job (a new continuation after a later reconnect reconciles again). **Monitor disposition (manifest mode):** the shipped ACK-timeout monitor (`fulfillment_listener_service.py:872-922`, armed at `trust_websocket.py:1373-1402`; shipped cancel sites are `handle_chunk` `:220` and complete/error `trust_websocket.py:1258` only) is **additionally** cancelled — a manifest-mode change — on every metadata message and on every refused chunk, and re-armed only when an ACK is sent, so a device waiting `TRANSFER_RETRY_AFTER_S` on `listener_busy` can never be aborted by it; in manifest mode `abort_transfer` (`:730-755`) takes the per-order lock, resets unverified members only, never touches a verified object, and unlinks nothing (there is no `.partial` file). The checks: members whose part receipts are **complete** but `verified_at` is null are `HEAD`ed on their final key first — present → enqueued to the finalizer for re-hash (a crash after `CompleteMultipartUpload`/`PutObject`); absent → the open-upload rule below applies; the order's staging state must be `active` (§3.7; `purging` → refused with `listener_busy`, `purged` → a fresh generation is minted, every member reset, and the session starts empty); for every member with `verified_at`, `HEAD` on its final key must return the recorded ETag and size, else the member is reset (D5 (c), now O(verified members) HEAD calls at session start — 21,000 Class B operations at the trigger scale, ≈ $0.008); for every unverified member with **incomplete** part receipts (continuation only — a resume has none), its persisted `upload_id` must appear in `list_multipart_uploads(prefix)` (fetched once per generation prefix, paginated, membership checked in memory; else the member is reset), and every open upload under the generation prefix that is not a persisted `upload_id` is aborted (**invariant: at most one open multipart upload per member key**, adopted by persisted id, never by key alone). **The orphan test is evaluated against durable state only** — the DB rows, never the replica's in-memory registry — so a replica restart that loses the part buffer never aborts an upload whose parts are receipted: the buffer is gone, the `upload_id` and part receipts are not, and the next part uploads under the same id. `verified_member_indices` is computed only from members that passed the HEAD.

### 3.3 Chunk arrival: part window
The frozen chunk plan (D5) additionally groups each member's chunks into **parts** of `TRANSFER_PART_BYTES` (8 MiB = 128 chunks of 65,536 bytes; the last part of a member is shorter; a member of ≤ `TRANSFER_PART_BYTES` is one part uploaded by `PutObject`). Per open session the listener keeps **one in-memory part buffer** with a per-chunk bitmap. Order of operations per chunk: validate `chunk_index`/`member_index`/`byte_offset`/length against the plan → verify `chunk_sha256` → look up the chunk receipt: present and equal → acknowledged, nothing written; present and different → refused; absent and the chunk belongs to the **current part** (the lowest part of the lowest unverified member with no part receipt, or the part currently buffered) → copied into the buffer at its offset (a resend within the buffer overwrites identical bytes); absent and the chunk belongs to a **later** part → refused with `out_of_window` (session stays open; the device re-sends metadata and resumes, §2). **Deliberate deviation from D5, stated for the vote:** D5 tolerates arbitrary out-of-order arrival; this amendment tolerates it only within the current part, because a multipart part must be complete before it can be written. The plan-order sender never triggers `out_of_window` except after a member reset (below). **ACK cadence (manifest mode):** the shipped rule acknowledges at *global* chunk indices ≡ 3 (mod 4) (`fulfillment_listener_service.py:276-284`), which is correct only for a stream that starts at index 0; a re-stream after a metadata replay starts at the first unverified member's first index `m`, and the shipped sender's validator expects the ACK for its window `[m, m+3]` to carry `acked_through_index ≥ m+3` (`aim-data:app/services/fulfillment_service.py:428-464`, `:492-502`). So in manifest mode the listener ACKs **relative to the session's stream origin**: it records `origin` = the index of the first chunk received after each metadata response and acknowledges the chunk whose index satisfies `(chunk_index − origin) % 4 == 3`, plus the plan's last chunk (`acked_through_index` = that index) — index arithmetic, so a duplicate or a re-sent window never shifts the boundary — which is exactly the plan-order sender's window boundary whatever `m` is. An ACK is sent as soon as the chunks are accepted into the buffer — backpressure, not durability. Part boundaries are **not** in general window-aligned (a member's parts start at its own first index), so the rule is stated without alignment: **the response to the chunk that fills a part is returned after that part commits**, because the shipped serial handler (`app/api/v1/endpoints/trust_websocket.py:776-777`, `:1282`) returns `handle_chunk`'s result; whether that response is an ACK or a plain per-chunk result, the sender's next ACK wait cannot be satisfied before it. This is why the chunk-path deadline (§3.1) is bounded inside the sender's 30 s ACK timeout; a part commit that exceeds it answers the chunk with a session-level error, the shipped sender fails the session, and the order resumes by a new session (verified members retained by the §3.2 HEAD check; that session's unverified receipts swept and re-streamed). **Refusals are immediate:** a refusal (`out_of_window`, `member_reset`, plan violation, session-level error) is the chunk's own response regardless of window position — the shipped listener already answers its per-chunk refusals that way (`fulfillment_listener_service.py:217`/`:224`/`:228`/`:239`/`:244`) — and the chunk D sender consumes it whenever it arrives (§2). **Member reset during an open session:** when a member is reset (§3.1 error class, §3.4 mismatch, lease), the listener answers the device's next chunk for that member with `member_reset`; the device re-sends metadata and resumes (§2). When the buffer's bitmap is full, the listener calls `UploadPart` (creating the multipart upload on the member's first part and persisting its `upload_id`; or `PutObject` for a single-part member), and on success inserts, **in one transaction**, the part receipt with the returned ETag and the part's chunk receipts, advancing `bytes_received` and `chunks_received` on `transfer_session_members`. **A chunk receipt therefore still implies durable bytes** (in the store, under an open multipart upload or a completed object) — stated precisely as a **reconcile-before-use invariant**, not cross-store atomicity: a receipt records an observed successful store write; an out-of-band loss afterwards (a lifecycle or manual abort, a deleted object) can leave receipts pointing at nothing until the next use, and every use of receipts is preceded by a store check that resets the member on loss — resume (§3.2(b): `upload_id` in `list_multipart_uploads`, HEAD on verified keys), finalization (§3.4: `NoSuchUpload`/`NoSuchPart` on `CompleteMultipartUpload`), completion (§3.5: HEAD), and the listener's own aborts (§3.7, lease), which delete the member's receipts in the same step. Receipts are therefore never *used* for `verified_member_indices`, finalization or delivery without a store check that would expose the loss; with a dead scheduler and a silent sender the stale receipts simply wait for the next message or resume, where they are reset. A crash before `UploadPart` returns loses only the buffer: no receipts exist for those chunks; on the continuation (metadata replay, §3.2) the member is reported unverified and the device re-streams it from its first chunk; chunks of already-receipted parts are acknowledged idempotently without any store write, so a re-stream costs wire but not store. **A replay ACK is transport idempotence, not a durability certification:** it says the listener holds a receipt for those bytes, and an out-of-band loss after the last store check is repaired at the next checkpoint — finalization (`NoSuchUpload`/`NoSuchPart`), the completion job's HEAD, or the next reconciliation — never later than before `verified_at`, `delivered` or a grant. Per-chunk work is O(1) (one bitmap set and one memcpy; one indexed receipt lookup) and per-part work is one store call and one transaction of 129 inserted rows plus one row update. Zero-byte member: one zero-length `PutObject` (matches the shipped sender's single empty chunk).

### 3.4 Member finalization
**Finalization runs off the ACK critical path:** each open session owns one background finalizer task (same replica, same process as the websocket handler; its store calls run on the finalizer executor, §3.1, so they can never occupy a chunk-path worker); when a member's part receipts equal its expected part count, `handle_chunk` only enqueues the member and returns. The finalizer, under the per-order lock for its row writes only, calls `CompleteMultipartUpload` with the recorded ETags in order (or the `PutObject` already completed it), then **streams the object back from the store and hashes it** (budget `TRANSFER_STORE_TIMEOUT_S`, one retry; a 2 GiB read takes minutes and never delays an ACK); equal to the manifest `sha256` → `verified_at`, the object's ETag and size are recorded on `transfer_session_members`; different → `DeleteObject`, receipts and part receipts for that member deleted, counters reset, member reported unverified (D5's repair rule; the device streams it again). The store read is the equivalent of D5's "hash from wherever the bytes are": it verifies what the store holds, not what the listener sent. `TRANSFER_MAX_MEMBER_BYTES` (2 GiB) → at most 256 parts per member, well under R2's 10,000. Reconciliation (b) of D5 becomes: any member whose part receipts are complete and whose `verified_at` is null is enqueued to the finalizer on the next message for the session (bounded to that message's member plus members left in that state by a crash) — the message itself is answered at once; a crash between `CompleteMultipartUpload`/`PutObject` and `verified_at` is repaired by the §3.2 HEAD-first rule (object present → re-hash and record; absent → the member is reset). **Complete vs finalization:** see §3.5 — `vai.fulfillment.complete` never blocks on finalization or on the completion sweep; it answers within `TRANSFER_FINALIZE_WAIT_S` either with the grant or with `finalizing`. **Absolute upload lease:** a member's open upload older than `TRANSFER_UPLOAD_MAX_AGE_S` (3 days from `create_multipart`) is aborted by the listener on the next message or sweep and the member reset — so a live-but-slow transfer is re-streamed member by member rather than ever meeting the 8-day lifecycle rule (§3.7), and D5's 6-hour idle TTL keeps its meaning.

### 3.5 Completion transaction
**The completion job runs behind the `finalizing` channel, never on the response path.** On `vai.fulfillment.complete` (scalars as D5) the listener, within `TRANSFER_FINALIZE_WAIT_S` (20 s, inside the shipped sender's single 30 s complete wait, `aim-data:app/services/fulfillment_service.py:236-248`): if a completion job for this session has already **succeeded**, answers idempotently with the existing grant (the shipped `:329-340` behaviour); if one has **failed** on a member, answers the refusal naming the unverified members (the device re-streams them); otherwise starts the job if none is running and answers **`finalizing`**. The device re-sends complete every `TRANSFER_RETRY_AFTER_S` within `TRANSFER_COMPLETE_RETRY_BUDGET_S` (§2). The **completion job** (finalizer executor, §3.1) waits for the session's finalizer to drain, requires every `transfer_session_members` row to carry `verified_at`, then — **outside any DB transaction, with the per-order lock not held** — performs one `HEAD` per member (batched, concurrency 16 on the sweep executor; AC-D4 bounds the 21,000-member sweep) comparing ETag and size to the recorded values; any miss resets that member (under the lock) and the job fails naming it. Then it writes `order_delivery_members` (with the final key as `staged path`, the recorded `object_etag`/`object_size`, `state = pending`) **unlocked, in batches of `TRANSFER_LOCKED_BATCH_ROWS`**, idempotent on `(order_id, index)`; then flips them `pending → delivered` **in batches of the same size, each batch under the lock with a `receiving` re-check and the lock released between batches** (the order is not yet `delivered`, so the buyer path cannot observe a partial set); and only then, in one short transaction under the per-order lock (`lock_timeout` 5 s, retried by the job), re-reads the session (still `receiving`), requires the count of `delivered` rows to equal the member count and every `verified_at`/recorded ETag to be unchanged, marks the order `delivered`, marks the session complete and issues the grant — three rows, never more. **Crash recovery:** a job that dies at any point leaves idempotent `pending`/`delivered` rows and an undelivered order; the next `complete` replay restarts the job, which re-runs the HEAD sweep and resumes the writes from the rows present; an order the device never returns to — whether it died before, during or after the `delivered` flips — is purged by rule (iii) with all of its member rows once its session is terminal and the abandoned TTL has passed; **The job's state is durable:** `transfer_sessions.completion_state ∈ {none, running, succeeded, failed}`, set under the per-order lock by the job (`running` when it starts, `failed` naming the members when a check fails, `succeeded` in the final transaction). The chunk path reads it and refuses `finalizing` while `running`, so the member set cannot change under the job; a metadata replay answers `listener_busy` while `running`, the grant when `succeeded` (D5 idempotent replay for a `delivered` order) and the indices when `failed`; the chunk D sender re-sends metadata on either refusal (§2); the unmodified sender fails the session, and the job still runs to its outcome. **Residual after issuance (§3.6):** an object deleted out of band after the issuance HEAD and before the buyer's GET makes that URL answer a store 404 for its TTL; the next request for the member finds the loss and marks it `unavailable` — no design that issues an independent store's URL can close this window, and it is stated rather than claimed away. The shipped ACK-timeout monitor (`fulfillment_listener_service.py:872-922`; armed at `trust_websocket.py:1400`; shipped cancel sites `fulfillment_listener_service.py:220` and `trust_websocket.py:1258`; manifest-mode additions in §3.2) is cancelled by the complete message and stays cancelled while the job runs. **Deliberate deviation from D5, stated for the vote:** D5 re-hashes every member a second time at completion; against the store that would read the whole set twice (up to 64 GiB) from R2 for no additional guarantee, because each member was already hashed *from the store* at finalization, its key names the expected hash, the store's objects are immutable except by an overwrite under our own bucket-scoped credential, and the ETag recorded at finalization changes on any overwrite. The `HEAD` check detects deletion, replacement and truncation; it does not detect an adversary with our credential, which no re-hash would either. **Caveat, stated:** an object's ETag is store metadata — for a multipart object it is derived from the parts' MD5s (R2 behaviour UNVERIFIED until AC-D1) — so equivalence to a SHA-256 re-hash holds only up to MD5 collision resistance against a writer that already holds our credential; that is inside the excluded trust boundary. Decided by Max as D-A1 (§6); the fallback, recorded for the record, is a second streamed re-hash of every member (same code path as §3.4) at ≈ 2× store read time and no design change.

### 3.6 Buyer path (interim, shipped by chunk D)
As D5 — one order-scoped grant per order and purchased version; entitlement per set; the order endpoint returns the member list — with the fetch restated: each member address is `GET /api/v1/orders/{order_id}/members/{member_index}` under the grant (header `X-Download-Token`, the shipped convention at `fulfillment_download.py:43-58`, plus the authenticated buyer); the endpoint validates grant, **grant remaining validity ≥ `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` + 60 s** (else refused `grant_expiring`: the buyer renews the grant, which counts as one download as D5 says, so an issued URL can never outlive its grant), order `delivered`, staging state `active` (§3.7), member in `order_delivery_members`, then — **reconcile before use, the delivery leg of §3.3** — performs a `HEAD` on the member's final key (chunk-path per-call budget, 5/10 s, no retry) and compares ETag and size to the values recorded in `order_delivery_members`: a miss (deleted, replaced, truncated out of band after `delivered`) marks that member `unavailable` on `order_delivery_members`, answers `member_unavailable` (a named terminal state: the order page shows "file unavailable — support notified" and a support ticket is opened with the order id and index; no member repair exists after `delivered`, D5 immutability), and consumes no budget; on a match it **re-checks the grant's remaining validity** (still ≥ `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` + 60 s — the HEAD is bounded by 15 s and clock skew by the 60 s margin, so an issued URL can never outlive its grant) and the buyer's byte budget, and answers **`302` to a presigned R2 GET** with TTL `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` (300 s), `response-content-disposition: attachment; filename*=UTF-8''<basename(relative_path), RFC 5987 percent-encoded>` plus an ASCII `filename=` fallback (RFC 6266, the workspace amendment W-D4 rule), `response-content-type: application/octet-stream`; the redirect response itself carries `Cache-Control: no-store` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`. ai.market never proxies member bytes (R2 serves them, no egress fee); a presigned URL is single-member, expires in minutes, and discloses nothing but that member: the key carries the order id, generation, member index and hash, and the disposition parameter carries the basename of the buyer's own paid file — no seller or buyer identity and no directory path. The grant margin `TRANSFER_GRANT_MARGIN_S` (60 s) and the meter retention `METER_RETENTION_S` (25 h) are §3.8 rows. **Budget — restating D5's "bounded by a per-client byte budget" honestly, as this amendment's change to D5:** `DOWNLOAD_GRANT_BYTES_PER_HOUR` (deferred by Gate 2 §6; renamed in meaning, not in name) is an **issuance** budget: the sum of the sizes of members for which a presigned URL was issued to one **buyer, across all of that buyer's grants and orders**, in the trailing hour, may not exceed it. **Window mechanism:** a **tumbling hourly bucket** — `buyer_download_meter(buyer_id, window_start, bytes_issued)`, `window_start` = the current hour floored, one row per buyer per hour; "trailing hour" therefore means the current clock hour, and a buyer can issue up to 2× the value across an hour edge (stated; acceptable for a soft request-volume guard over the buyer's own paid members). **Ordering:** the presigned URL is generated first (a local HMAC, no network), then in one short transaction the bucket row — `UNIQUE (buyer_id, window_start)`, created by an upsert (`INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`) and then locked `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, so a concurrent first request for the same hour serializes on the row rather than on a missing one — `bytes_issued + size ≤ DOWNLOAD_GRANT_BYTES_PER_HOUR` is checked and the size added, and only then is the redirect returned — so concurrent requests cannot overshoot, a failed presign consumes no budget, and a response lost after the commit is counted (conservative). Rows older than 25 hours are deleted by the §3.7 scheduler job (the table holds at most one row per active buyer per hour). Value **192 GiB** = 3 × `TRANSFER_MAX_TOTAL_BYTES`. What it does *not* bound, stated plainly: a presigned URL is replayable until it expires and R2 keeps no per-URL counter that ai.market reads, so **bytes actually served** are additionally bounded only by `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` × the client's bandwidth per issued URL (at 1 Gbit/s, ≈ 37 GB per URL per 300 s — and only of a member the buyer has paid for). R2 charges no egress, so the residual exposure is R2 request volume, not money. If a served-byte bound is required, the buyer path must be a backend stream that reads from R2 and meters bytes per buyer (GLM R1 BETTER) — decided by Max as **D-A3** for the presign model (§6, Event Ledger `a3a64f39`); the backend-stream alternative is recorded for the record. The legacy single-file download at `fulfillment_download.py` is byte-identical. The order endpoint returns `delivered` rows (index, basename, size, hash, address) and `unavailable` rows (index, basename, size, hash, `state`, no address) and never `pending` rows; the order page (`ai-market-frontend app/dashboard/orders/[id]/page.tsx`, Gate 2 §2) renders a download control for the former and "file unavailable — support notified" for the latter. Gate 1 D5 reserves the buyer's fetch shape (per member or archive) to S1711; this per-member presigned fetch is the interim shape chunk D ships, and S1711 may supersede it.

### 3.7 Sweeps and retention
**Durable staging state (new, the D5 per-order lock made durable across deferred deletes):** a row `order_staging(order_id, generation UUID, state ∈ {active, purging, purged}, state_changed_at)` is created with the first manifest-mode session for an order; every key of the order carries its `generation`. Session creation (§3.2), chunk handling, completion and grant issuance/renewal (§3.6) all read it under the per-order lock and proceed only on `active`; `purging` refuses (session: `listener_busy` with retry-after; grant: `503` retry-after — the shipped grant path is unchanged for legacy orders); `purged` on an abandoned order lets the next metadata mint a fresh generation and start empty; `purged` on a delivered order is terminal for downloads (retention over — D5's `max(delivered_at + 24 h, last grant expiry)` plus one presign TTL has elapsed, so no grant and no issued URL can still be valid). One scheduler job (Celery beat, `app/tasks/multi_file_versions.py` beside chunk C's sweeps, Gate 2 R10) runs the shipped single-file routines unchanged and, over the `orders/` prefix only, the three D5 rules, each as **CAS first, delete second**: under the per-order lock the sweep re-checks its predicate and CAS-sets `active → purging` (capturing `generation`); after the lock is released it deletes the captured generation's keys and aborts its open uploads; then CAS-sets `purging → purged`. A delete that fails leaves `purging`, and the next run completes it; a delete can never touch a newer generation because new keys carry a new UUID. The rules: (i) `delivered` orders — after `max(delivered_at + 24 h, expiry of the last valid grant) + TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S`, with no grant currently valid (a grant cannot be issued or renewed once the CAS has run, and §3.6 refuses a presign in a grant's final `TRANSFER_PRESIGN_TTL_S` + 60 s, so no live URL can point into a purged generation); (ii) sessions past `TRANSFER_SESSION_TTL_S` (idle) — under the lock mark the session `TIMED_OUT` (the shipped mark, `:811`/`:832`, now with a caller), then **reset every unverified member of that session** (chunk and part receipts deleted) in batches of `TRANSFER_LOCKED_BATCH_ROWS` with the lock released between batches (a chunk for a `TIMED_OUT` session is refused as shipped, `:223-224`, so no writer competes), then abort those members' open uploads by their persisted `upload_id`s; verified objects are retained for resume and the staging state stays `active`; (iii) abandoned orders past `ABANDONED_ORDER_TTL_S` — the predicate is **authoritative order state only**: no `receiving` session and `orders.delivered_at IS NULL` (the member rows' states are not consulted: a completion job that died before, during or after its `delivered` flips leaves an undelivered order, and a delivered order is excluded by `delivered_at`) — CAS `active → purging`, delete every `order_delivery_members` row of the order, abort every open upload under the generation, `delete_prefix`, CAS `→ purged`. Members whose open upload exceeds `TRANSFER_UPLOAD_MAX_AGE_S` (§3.4) are reset and aborted by the same job, which also deletes `buyer_download_meter` rows older than 25 hours (§3.6). **Backstop:** an R2 bucket lifecycle rule "abort incomplete multipart uploads after 8 days" on the `orders/` prefix, set by Max in the dashboard as a §8 checkpoint. It is unreachable for any upload the listener still considers live (the 3-day lease is enforced by the listener on every message, not only by the scheduler), it cannot delete a completed object, and if it ever does abort an upload the listener learns it as `NoSuchUpload` and resets the member (§3.1) — so even a dead scheduler leaves nothing that the reconcile-before-use invariant (§3.3) does not reset before the receipts are used. Immutability from `delivered` holds: after that commit nothing writes under the order's generation prefix except sweep (i).

## 5. Risks
- **Part buffer in memory:** a replica restart loses at most one part per open session; bounded by `TRANSFER_MAX_OPEN_SESSIONS`; recovery is member-level re-streaming, which is slower than D5's chunk-level repair for members > 8 MiB — accepted because it needs no wire change.
- **Twice the bytes through R2:** every member is written once and read once at finalization (Class A writes ≈ $4.50/million, Class B reads ≈ $0.36/million; no egress fee). At the trigger scale (21,000 members, mostly single-part) ≈ 21,000 writes + 21,000 reads + 21,000 HEADs ≈ $0.10 per delivery, plus ≈ $0.008 per resume.
- **Presigned URLs are bearer tokens for five minutes** for one paid member, replayable within the TTL; mitigated by TTL, the per-buyer issuance budget, `no-store` and the buyer-authenticated redirect; the served-byte exposure is stated in §3.6 and is Max's D-A3.
- **Lifecycle backstop is dashboard-configured** (not code): recorded as a §8 checkpoint and in `listing-asset-store.md`; the listener's own 3-day lease makes correctness independent of it.
- **Deployment:** the Celery service that runs the sweeps needs the four `LISTING_ASSET_*` variables (today mirrored only to `ai-market-backend`, `listing-asset-store.md`); without them the sweeps no-op and staging leaks (cost, not data loss) — §8 checkpoint.
